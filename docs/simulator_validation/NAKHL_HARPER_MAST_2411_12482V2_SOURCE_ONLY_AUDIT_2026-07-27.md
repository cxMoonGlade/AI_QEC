### Stabilizer-basis coefficient state

The intended representation is

\[
\lvert\psi\rangle
=\sum_{i=1}^{2^N}\nu_iD_{\hat i}\lvert\phi\rangle,
\]

where the coefficients are stored as an ordinary MPS over the binary label
\(\hat i\). Clifford gates update the tableau basis and leave this MPS
unchanged. This is an STN coefficient-basis representation; it is not the
CAMPS/GCAMPS factorization \(C\lvert\mathrm{MPS}\rangle\).

The intended basis cannot be reconstructed literally from Eq. (B2), because
that equation calls \(\phi_j\) a row of the stabilizer tableau while the main
text, Appendix A, and the symbol \(D_{\hat i}\) identify destabilizer
products. Products of stabilizers acting on \(\lvert\phi\rangle\) would not
generate the full basis. The operation replay therefore records the intended
destabilizer reading and separately rejects Eq. (B2) as a self-contained
definition.

### Non-Clifford update

The source decomposes

\[
U=\sum_i c_iD_{\hat d_i}S_{\hat s_i}.
\]

For two terms, Eqs. (B7)--(B9) factor one Pauli term and express the remaining
action as a multi-qubit rotation on coefficient indices. This is a formal
algebraic update. The support is determined by tableau commutation labels,
not by physical geometric locality; no theorem states that it remains local
in the MPS order or in a two-dimensional PEPS.

### MAST injection loop

Figure 1 replaces a \(T\) gate by preparation of a magic state, a Clifford
injection gadget, a measurement, and an \(S\) correction for the displayed
branch. The MAST section says the measurement result can be predetermined in
simulation and the actual projection delayed until the end. Thus Clifford
evolution and magic-state preparation are cheap in this representation, and
the expensive coefficient-MPS entangling operation is moved to a final
projection stage.

This is not a general instrument loop. The source neither enumerates the two
injection branches with their Born masses nor constructs a distribution over
ordered histories. It uses the gate identity to choose the corrected
injection outcome relevant to the simulated unitary.

### Selective projection

Appendix B.4 intends the physical update

\[
\lvert\psi_p^{\rm raw}\rangle
=\frac{I+pO}{2}\lvert\psi\rangle,
\]

then maps it to an MPS operation, performs the standard tableau update, and
renormalizes the MPS. The section does not state
\((1+p\langle O\rangle)/2\) as a branch probability and does not preserve the
pre-normalization norm as a ledgered branch mass.

The coefficient formula cannot be accepted literally. Eq. (B10) switches
from amplitudes \(\nu_i\) to undefined \(c_i\) and prints
\(D_{\hat i\cdot\hat b}\), although no binary-label product with that meaning
was defined. Eq. (B11) introduces \(D_{\hat n}\) without defining
\(\hat n\) in this derivation. Appendix C then rewrites the projection with
scalar factors that contradict Eq. (C1). These anomalies do not erase the
standard physical projector, but they prevent this source from serving as a
standalone implementation-faithful coefficient-update specification.

### Random-circuit cost argument

For the source's ensemble, each layer contains a uniformly sampled
\(N\)-qubit Clifford followed by a \(T\) gate. MAST adds one magic-state
ancilla per \(T\) resource. Appendix C analyzes whether the first
destabilizer row anticommuting with a projected magic-register \(Z_i\) lies in
the original data-register part of the coefficient MPS. Under its random
tableau model, many projections do not entangle the coefficient MPS while
\(t\lesssim N\). Figure 2 reports the maximum bond reached per instance,
averaged over 1000 instances, and separates:

- Region A, \(t\lesssim N\), where the caption reports average
  \(\chi\le3\);
- Region B, \(N\lesssim t\lesssim1.5N\), with exponential MAST bond growth;
- Region C, where MAST and STN reach \(\chi=2^{N/2}\).

This result is ensemble- and observable-specific. The source explicitly says
the simplified Appendix-C model only accounts for a primary
\(\chi=2\rightarrow4\) growth mechanism and omits instance-specific tensor
network compression. It also states that efficient local expectation values
do not imply efficient general output sampling.

## Source-local anomalies

1. **Coefficient-basis indexing.** Equations (A2), (B1), and the main Eq. (1)
   enumerate \(i=1,\ldots,2^N\), while \(\hat i=(s_1,\ldots,s_N)\) is an
   \(N\)-bit binary label. The source does not explain how the all-zero label
   is represented or how \(2^N\) is encoded by \(N\) bits. This is an indexing
   convention gap, not evidence of an extra basis state.
2. **Equation (B2) generator identity.** The equation defines
   \(D_{\hat i}=\prod_j\phi_j^{\hat i_j}\) and immediately calls \(\phi_j\) a
   row of the stabilizer tableau. The surrounding text says \(D_{\hat i}\)
   is defined from destabilizer rows. Those two statements are incompatible
   as literal definitions.
3. **Equation (B10) coefficient and label operation.** The displayed
   projection uses \(c_i\) where the state uses \(\nu_i\), and the subscript
   \(\hat i\cdot\hat b\) is not defined as a coefficient-basis combination.
4. **Equation (B11) undefined label.** \(D_{\hat n}\) appears without a
   definition of \(\hat n\) in Appendix B.4. The prose defines \(k\) as the
   first destabilizer row anticommuting with \(O\), but does not identify
   \(\hat n\) with a unit vector at \(k\).
5. **Equations (C1)--(C2) normalization.** Equation (C1) defines its last
   expression as \(\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert_k\mathcal R\). Using the
   same line's definition, replacing the one-qubit projector by
   \((I+Z_k)/2\) gives a factor \(1/2\), whereas Eq. (C2) prints
   \(1/(4\sqrt2)\). The subsequent cancellation inherits that mismatch.
6. **Appendix-C finite-\(n\) probability.** The text begins from
   \(\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb F_2)\) but then counts nonzero vectors in
   \(\mathbb F_2^n\) and later writes \(\mathrm{Sp}(n;\mathbb F_2)\).
   Therefore the exact claim
   \(p_{i,j}(n)=2^{n-1}/(2^n-1)\) has no consistent printed dimension
   convention. The asymptotic approach to one half is not sufficient to
   repair the finite-\(n\) statement silently.
7. **Figure 8 caption.** The caption describes the no-extra-ancilla case as
   another four-\(T\) decomposition, while the figure legend, Appendix E,
   and Fig. 7 identify that case as the seven-\(T\), no-extra-ancilla
   decomposition. The plotted comparison is read from the legend and
   surrounding text, not from that caption phrase.

## Project application

MAST is direct adjacent prior art for a hybrid stabilizer--tensor-network
simulator that handles extensive non-Clifford resources and a selective
projection primitive. It therefore blocks any broad claim that combining a
tableau-like stabilizer structure, tensor-network coefficients, non-Clifford
residual updates, or projective measurement is itself new.

Its representation and algorithm nevertheless differ from the proposed
CAPEPS route:

1. MAST inherits the STN expansion in a destabilizer-generated stabilizer
   basis; CAPEPS proposes a leading physical Clifford frame
   \(C\lvert\mathrm{PEPS}\rangle\).
2. MAST's concrete coefficient backend is one-dimensional MPS; no
   physical-lattice PEPS residual is defined.
3. MAST converts \(T\) gates to magic-state injection and defers the
   expensive projections; CAPEPS must process the actual
   measurement--reset schedule in temporal order if it is to preserve a
   complete Record law.
4. MAST benchmarks random \(T\)-doped Clifford and Hidden Bit Shift circuits;
   it does not simulate XZZX syndrome extraction.
5. MAST reports coefficient bond and selected runtime, not conditional-state
   fidelity, raw-history mass conservation, detector Record-TV, peak memory,
   or a matched full-PEPS/twirled-tableau comparison.

The source's conclusion explicitly distinguishes MAST from CAMPS and says the
cited CAMPS method uses an optimization subroutine. The later GCAMPS
\(C\lvert\mathrm{MPS}\rangle\) surface-code work is established by a separate
source, arXiv:2605.29514v1; it must not be back-attributed to this MAST paper.

## Competing evidence and kill conditions

The original STN version of record, Masot-Llima and Garcia-Saez,
*Phys. Rev. Lett.* 133, 230601, provides the cleaner source for the
stabilizer-basis representation and outcome-resolved Pauli measurement. This
MAST paper explicitly builds on that source and does not supersede its
measurement derivation. The current MAST appendix's printed projection
anomalies are therefore not a basis for claiming a new exact instrument.

The following statements are killed unless a separate source or an
independent derivation and falsifier closes them:

1. MAST is a \(C\lvert\mathrm{MPS}\rangle\) or
   \(C\lvert\mathrm{PEPS}\rangle\) method;
2. its \(t\lesssim N\) result is a worst-case polynomial guarantee;
3. it provides efficient general Born sampling;
4. deferred injection projection preserves an arbitrary temporal
   measurement--reset Record law;
5. it specifies reset, raw branch masses, prefix masses, or detector folds;
6. its coefficient bond curves establish a full-PEPS resource advantage;
7. its Hidden Bit Shift benchmark validates XZZX QEC correctness; or
8. its printed Appendix-B/C formulas are implementation-ready without repair.
