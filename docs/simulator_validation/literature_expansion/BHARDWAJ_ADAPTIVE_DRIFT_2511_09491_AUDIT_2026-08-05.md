## Formula and reporting caveats found by visual verification

- Equation (7) prints the normalized rectangular-window magnitude response as
  `W^-1 |sin(pi*m*W/N)/sin(pi*m/N)|`, but Eq. (8) prints its cutoff equation without
  the `W^-1` factor. Equation (9), the stated `c(0.05) approximately 0.12`, and the reported
  `W_opt = 1228 +/- 42` behave as though a normalized response was intended. The source does
  not reconcile the printed forms, so Eq. (8) must not be transcribed as an unqualified exact rule.
- Equation (B6) prints the second-moment cross term with the same time index in both probability
  factors rather than displaying a distinct pair of indices. Equations (B7)–(B8) state the standard
  independent-Bernoulli variance. This record preserves the discrepancy rather than silently
  repairing the appendix derivation.
- Figures 10–11 show directional logical benefit and uncertainty bars but the prose supplies no
  tabulated numerical improvement ratio. Only the plotted, model-conditioned comparison is
  supportable.
- The full text names Stim as the circuit simulator but does not name or configure the decoder used
  for Figs. 9–11, and it gives no runtime, memory, or latency benchmark for the “very low
  computational resources” statement in the conclusion.

## Project application

This source can support a selective overview example of **adaptation to nonstationary drift**. The
four layers should be reported separately:

- temporal representation: prescribed time-indexed independent Pauli rates, usually a sum of
  sinusoids, not a memory state;
- QEC-facing abstraction: time-dependent probabilities on DEM graph edges estimated from detector
  firing and coincidence statistics;
- computation: rectangular sliding windows, least-squares spectral reconstruction or overlapping
  relative windows with Savitzky–Golay smoothing, followed by Stim simulation and an unnamed
  decoder;
- demonstrated reach: synthetic distance-3 repetition-code and rotated-surface-code memory tasks,
  with phenomenological and one repetition-code circuit-level model.

For the evidence section it supports a narrow statement that, in these prescribed drift
simulations, updating the DEM can improve logical performance relative to a stationary DEM. It does
not close a memory-aware decoding row, an experimental observation row, a microscopic attribution
row, a model-mismatch robustness row, or a transfer row. The paper’s use of syndrome **history** as
data must not be rewritten as a claim that the physical process has memory.

## Competing evidence and kill conditions

- The source itself kills a memory/non-Markovian promotion: Eq. (3) is stated for Markovian
  time-dependent noise, Appendix A assumes statistical independence across times, and Sec. V lists
  extension to non-Markovian noise as future work.
- The source itself kills an experimental-observation or device-attribution claim: all validation is
  simulated, the drift functions are prescribed, and experimental validation is an explicit next
  step.
- The phrase “can be applied to any quantum error-correcting code” in Sec. I is a method-scope
  assertion, not demonstrated transfer. The only code instances are distance-3 repetition and
  distance-3 rotated surface codes.
- “Robust across a wide range of error rates” in Sec. V refers to tests inside the same assumed model
  families. Any use as robustness to calibration error, non-Pauli noise, correlated events, or model
  misspecification is invalidated by the missing perturbation.
- A quantitative benefit ratio is killed by the absence of tabulated values and by the unspecified
  decoder implementation. The defensible claim is the direction and plotted scale within Figs. 10–11.
