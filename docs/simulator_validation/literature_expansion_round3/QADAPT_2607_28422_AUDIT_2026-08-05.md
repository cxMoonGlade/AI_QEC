## Project application

QAdapt is a useful coverage-validation source, but its scientific role is narrower than the title's
language about nonstationary hardware might suggest.

- **Memory-conditioned benefit:** the network consumes a multiround ordered detector tensor, but no
  reported contrast isolates access to temporal history or a declared memory-bearing state. The
  baseline and QAdapt differ in architecture, parameter count and training exposure. This source
  therefore does not close D1.
- **Population comparison:** the paper improves on event-selected evidence because it reports every
  cell of its fixed 110-point design grid and stated Willow cohorts. This is not a sampled
  population, and it does not establish that the cohorts exhaust the public release or preserve
  paired record-level outcomes. It is not the D2 minimum: uncertainty and a one-factor matched
  comparator are absent. The high-load subset also compares
  distance-7 and distance-9 synthetic configurations against a density threshold measured at the
  distinct distance-3, Z-basis, nine-round hardware operating point; the paper does not establish
  distributional equivalence between those settings.
- **Robustness:** the 110-point result is meaningful generic OOD evidence over static Pauli parameter
  changes. It is not robustness to an incorrect memory law, carrier lifetime or mixed temporal
  mechanism, and there is no controlled EWC ablation.
- **Transfer:** this source changes an unqualified statement that no decoder package has been
  evaluated on an external QEC hardware dataset without target-domain updating. The stronger label
  “strict zero-shot/frozen target-unseen transfer” is not independently auditable: T0 hardware
  provenance, target exposure during model selection, exact evaluated checkpoints and cohort
  filtering are not printed. It does not close cross-device, cross-code or memory-model transfer.
- **Temporal specificity:** HTNet's temporal branch learns local time-like structure in the detector
  record, which may include ordinary circuit propagation and repeated-measurement structure. The
  source does not identify those features with a physical memory mechanism. Offline continual
  learning across predefined noise tasks should not be described as online memory-state tracking.
- **Framework:** no new Section 3 approach row is required. QAdapt fits as a decoder-facing evidence
  bundle once the comparison records its generator, record interface, training/adaptation protocol,
  inference method and demonstrated reach. The adaptation protocol should be visible because it is
  the main confound in the comparison.

## Competing evidence and kill conditions

### Adjacent evidence

- Nayak et al. explicitly model a continuing latent quasiparticle field and compare a field-inferred
  decoder with a fixed-prior decoder, but their proposed-decoder result is synthetic and selected-event.
- AlphaQubit supplies multiround learned decoding on experimental records, but a learned-decoder gain
  is not automatically a temporal-memory ablation.
- Hockings et al. concern noise-aware calibration and decoder/model mismatch; that axis must remain
  distinct from memory-law misspecification unless the source varies a temporal model.

### Kill conditions

- Kill any claim that QAdapt demonstrates benefit from a known physical memory mechanism; its
  generator is a fixed circuit-level Pauli family and no mechanism is inferred on Willow.
- Kill any claim that the Willow comparison isolates detector history. No temporal-input or
  memory-state ablation is reported, and Ising-fast is not an otherwise identical comparator.
- Kill any claim that Q-EWC causes the improvement. The paper explicitly lacks comparisons with
  unregularized sequential fine-tuning, replay and joint mixed-noise training.
- Kill any significance or precision claim for LER differences. The source explicitly lacks LER
  confidence intervals and seed variation and calls paired-shot analysis future work.
- Kill any end-to-end latency or real-time deployment claim. Only residual-syndrome PyMatching is
  timed, without the neural, transfer or construction stages.
- Kill a public-reproducibility claim. Synthetic/device-mapped records are unavailable at submission,
  and the fixed text supplies neither source code nor the exact revision, checkpoint or seeds.
- Kill cross-code transfer. Only rotated-surface-code memories are evaluated; other codes and
  backends are future work.
- Kill strict target-unseen or hardware-to-hardware frozen transfer. The reported target is one
  external Willow release; T0's source hardware and target exposure during model selection are not
  disclosed, and the starting point is a simulated, hardware-mapped environment.
- Kill a claim that one identical checkpoint transfers across d=5, d=7 and d=9. The source does not
  state checkpoint-to-distance mapping.
- Kill online adaptation or online drift tracking. The online stage applies a fixed pre-decoder;
  online Fisher updates and drift detection are future work.

## Source-local anomalies and unresolved reporting fields

- The paper repeatedly calls the five multiplier values “retained” but does not identify an initial
  multiplier set or an exclusion rule in the full text.
- T0 and the task sequence are specified numerically, but synthetic training/evaluation shot counts,
  train/validation/test splits, evaluation round counts and checkpoint selection are not reported.
- The high-load threshold `0.13106` comes from a distance-3, Z-basis, nine-round hardware point and
  is applied to distance-7 and distance-9 synthetic configurations; the paper treats it as a load
  anchor and does not claim matched distributions.
- The Willow shot counts are stated, but the source does not say that these cohorts exhaust the
  public release, document filtering/exclusions, preserve paired outcomes, identify exact evaluated
  checkpoints or say whether one checkpoint serves both distances.
- The paper reports microseconds per round without naming the timing platform, execution settings,
  number of timing repetitions or dispersion.
- Section 7.4 says the evidence lacks LER confidence intervals, yet the headline gives percentage
  improvements to two decimal places. These remain descriptive point estimates.
- Appendix D is titled a reproducibility checklist and uses prospective instructions (“Record,”
  “Preserve,” “Report,” “Regenerate,” “Verify”); it does not supply those artifacts or values.
