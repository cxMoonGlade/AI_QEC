## Exact comparison and scale findings

- In the high-leakage model (`p=p_l=5e-4`), Fig. 3 reports `Lambda=2.12 +/- 0.00`
  without adaptivity and `3.85 +/- 0.03` with adaptivity. The archived distance-17 rows contain
  2,835 versus 11 failures in 10,000,000 shots.
- In the low-leakage model (`p=1e-3`, `p_l=1e-4`), Fig. 3 reports `Lambda=3.23 +/- 0.01`
  and `3.95 +/- 0.02`. The distance-17 rows contain 139 versus 24 failures in 10,000,000
  shots.
- The comparison is population-level over the declared stochastic generator, not an event-selected
  case study. Its strongest residual matching ambiguity is whether both decoder arms saw identical
  per-shot records.
- On XCVU19P at distance 17, Table 3 gives 0.460/0.622 microseconds per round for
  non-adaptive LL/HL and 0.637/0.676 for adaptive LL/HL. The distance-17 implementation uses
  251,963 logic LUTs (6.17%) and 252,736 flip-flops (3.09%).
- On ZCU111 at distance 17, the four times are 0.558, 0.754, 0.773 and 0.820 microseconds;
  57.66% of logic LUTs and 29.75% of flip-flops are used.
- Figure 6's PyMatching comparison is deliberately leakage-free and uses a standard non-wiggling
  schedule. It supports baseline decoder benchmarking (thresholds about 0.55% unweighted hardware
  LCD, 0.65% weighted software LCD and 0.70% PyMatching), not leakage-memory benefit or robustness.

## Project application

Ziad is a useful Section 5 decoder-benefit example only if its evidence layers remain explicit:

- **Memory-bearing representation:** a simulated classical leakage register, physically motivated
  by a long-lived out-of-subspace carrier but not a qutrit density-matrix evolution.
- **QEC-facing interface:** ordinary detector syndrome plus a separate measurement-derived leakage
  herald; the adaptive decoder receives pre-grown edges derived from this side information.
- **Computation:** a distributed unweighted Union-Find-like decoder in FPGA logic. The modified
  Stim generator and precomputation tooling are separate from the deployed decoding engine.
- **Demonstrated repeated-QEC reach:** synthetic rotated-surface-code memory experiments at odd
  distances 5--17 with patch wiggling, two fixed circuit-level noise regimes and 10 million shots
  per arm.
- **What the benefit establishes:** within this generator, exposing carrier-specific herald
  information to the same unweighted decoder greatly improves logical scaling. Adaptive
  decoder-engine cycle cost and total FPGA implementation resources are reported separately; the
  incremental adaptivity-engine time and resources are not isolated.
- **What it does not establish:** benefit on quantum-device records, syndrome-only discovery of
  memory, robustness to an incorrect leakage model, cross-code/device transfer or end-to-end
  adaptive latency.

Accordingly, the paper changes a blanket statement such as “there is no population-level hardware
memory-conditioned decoder comparison” into two narrower statements: there is a population-level
comparison implemented on decoder hardware for synthetic leakage records, but no such comparison
here on quantum-hardware records, and the archived artifacts do not prove identical-record pairing.

## Competing evidence and kill conditions

### Adjacent evidence

- Miao et al. and Google leakage-removal experiments use quantum hardware and logical/detector
  outcomes, but change the physical leakage carrier rather than isolating decoder access to a
  herald on fixed records.
- Nayak et al. isolate a latent-field-informed decoder on simulated records, but their proposed
  algorithms are reported for selected events rather than a full event population.
- AlphaQubit uses long syndrome histories and experimental records, but does not isolate a matched
  history-access ablation.

### Kill conditions

- Kill “hardware QEC data” or “device-demonstrated decoder benefit”: only the decoder is on FPGA;
  QEC records are simulated.
- Kill “same-record paired comparison”: the source fixes the same task/distribution and shot count
  but does not state record reuse, seeds or pairing.
- Kill “general memory-aware decoder”: the intervention is a leakage-herald-specific side channel
  with a two-round carrier lifetime under patch wiggling.
- Kill “robust to model misspecification”: both regimes remain inside the same matched leakage and
  partner-depolarisation family.
- Kill transfer to neutral atoms, ions or generic erasure. The peer-review response explicitly says
  the existing-edge simplification fails when the gate is erased without depolarising its partner.
- Kill “one million operations were run without error”: the `10^-6` window and `d=33` requirement
  are scaling projections; simulated/implemented distances end at 17.
- Kill end-to-end sub-microsecond adaptivity: reported decoder-engine values are derived from stage
  cycle averages and FPGA clock frequencies, while adaptivity-engine execution is explicitly
  unmeasured.
- Kill measured power/cost or ASIC evidence: LUT/FF utilisation is a proxy; ASIC is prospective.
- Kill full computational reproducibility: the authors say some simulation artifacts are
  proprietary, while archive inspection establishes specifically that the modified Stim source,
  RTL/implementation source and raw shot records are absent. The source does not identify which
  absent component carries the proprietary label.

## Source-local anomalies and qualifications

- Main PDF p. 3 twice refers to restoring/negating Eq. (2) while the visible exponential-suppression
  formula is Eq. (1); Eq. (2) later defines logical error per round for Fig. 6.
- Figure 3 prints numerical uncertainties on `Lambda`, but neither the article, SI, peer-review file
  nor archived CSV documents the fit or uncertainty estimator.
- The note's `operation_replay_status=complete` refers to circuit/task reconstruction, aggregate
  failure counts and timing arithmetic. It does not promote the undocumented `Lambda` fit or
  uncertainty procedure to replayable evidence.
- The public performance filename is `fpag_performance_data.csv`, not `fpga_...`.
- The abstract's “one million error-free quantum operations” compresses an extrapolated target
  error probability into operational language; the direct experiment consists of finite-window
  Monte Carlo sampling and FPGA decoding.
- The peer-review response p. 9 says graph reconfiguration takes fewer than 100 FPGA cycles and
  parenthetically equates this to 50 ns at 200 MHz. One 200-MHz cycle is 5 ns, so 100 cycles is
  500 ns; the parenthetical is not numerically implied by the stated upper bound and does not appear
  in the final article.
- Reviewers disagreed on reproducibility language. Reviewer 1 called the package non-reproducible
  because implementation source is missing; the authors answered that the circuits and algorithm
  description make the results reproducible “in principle.” The audit retains the concrete artifact
  boundary rather than adopting either label wholesale.
