## Project application

This source changes the status of the overview's control/reset lens. It supplies a concrete
memory-bearing carrier (long-lived transmon leakage), a measured physical intervention (DQLR), a
controlled same-code comparison and both record-level and logical outputs. The comparison is not
strictly matched in cycle duration, operations or detector definition.

- **Mechanism and attribution:** the source does more than associate an unexplained syndrome tail
  with a possible cause. It prepares leakage, observes its lifetime and transport, characterizes
  gate-level transport/phase pathways, and applies a targeted removal that suppresses the predicted
  temporal structures. This is strong leakage-specific causal evidence. It cannot be transferred to
  Google's later unexplained large bursts or to every form of temporal memory.
- **Control benefit:** DQLR is an active carrier-removal intervention, not post-selection and not a
  decoder change. The relevant public comparison is no reset versus MLR versus DQLR on common QEC
  circuits. Benefit must be reported together with extra cycle time, reset-error dependence and the
  XEB cost measurement; the equal-duration idle comparator applies to XEB, not to the QEC logical
  experiment.
- **Observation versus logical consequence:** leakage population, detector-correlation tails,
  detection probability and logical error are separately measured objects. Suppressing one does not
  erase the need to state which downstream metric was actually compared.
- **Scope:** the strongest large-code hardware result is a distance-21 bit-flip code, while the full
  surface-code hardware result is distance 3 and the distance-5/7 scaling result is simulated. The
  DQLR result is therefore not a general surface-code threshold demonstration.
- **Concept boundary:** the source studies a persistent physical state and multicycle correlations.
  It does not apply a process-tensor memory witness, a divisibility test or another criterion for
  strict quantum non-Markovianity.

## Competing evidence and kill conditions

### Competing or adjacent evidence

- Bultink et al. and Varbanov et al. infer leakage from records and use post-selection; neither is an
  active all-qubit carrier-removal comparison.
- McEwen et al., arXiv:2102.06131, supplies an earlier reset intervention in a bit-flip code. Miao et
  al. extends the intervention to every qubit in surface- and bit-flip-code circuits; the two papers
  share a substantial experimental lineage and must not be counted as independent replications.
- Google Quantum AI, arXiv:2408.13687v1, uses a later DQLR regime but explicitly leaves its newly
  observed large detector bursts unexplained. DQLR's success against leakage does not identify those
  events.
- Manabe et al., arXiv:2308.08186v2, compares no reset, MLR and DQLR in a tensor-network simulation;
  it supplies a computational cross-check and approximation study, not another hardware test.

### Kill conditions

- Kill any claim that all temporal correlations in the experiment are uniquely leakage: the source
  itself retains crosstalk and within-cycle residuals in the interpretation of longer edges.
- Kill any claim that DQLR is cost-free: it adds operations and time, depends on a preceding reset,
  and has a measured XEB error distribution.
- Kill any claim that the measured distance-3 surface-code comparison establishes asymptotic
  suppression; distance-5/7 scaling is simulated under a hypothetical error model.
- Kill any claim that DQLR proves memory-aware decoding benefit: the decoder information boundary is
  not the manipulated factor.
- Kill any claim of device-independent or code-independent transfer: all hardware data come from one
  Sycamore processor family and the interventions are separately instantiated.
- Kill any statement equating leakage persistence with strict quantum non-Markovianity.
