# Full-text review — Masot-Llima and Garcia-Saez, “Stabilizer Tensor Networks”

## Source identity [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-source-identity
Source locator: Article PDF page 1, title block, publication history, and DOI line; official Supplemental Material PDF page 1, matching title and author block
PDF page: 1
Claim: The source of record is Masot-Llima and Garcia-Saez's article published as *Physical Review Letters* 133, 230601 on December 3, 2024, together with its official supplemental material.

The article reports receipt on April 9, revision on September 17, and
acceptance on October 24, 2024. The separate audit packet binds both the
article and official supplement by SHA-256.

## Coefficient-state representation [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-coefficient-representation
Source locator: Article Fig. 1(b), Eq. (2), and surrounding Main results paragraph, PDF page 2
PDF page: 2
Claim: The source represents a physical state by amplitudes \(\nu_i\) in a tableau-defined stabilizer basis \(\mathcal B(S,D)\), with the amplitude state \(\lvert\nu\rangle\) stored in a tensor network.

The basis states are generated by products of destabilizers acting on a
stabilizer state. The article describes the coefficient tensor network as the
object that stores the amplitudes rather than the stabilizer tableau itself.

## Stabilizer-basis completeness [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-basis-completeness
Source locator: Supplemental Material Sec. I.A, Lemma 1 and Eqs. (1)--(3), PDF page 1
PDF page: 1
Claim: Supplemental Lemma 1 states and proves that the destabilizer-generated basis states are normalized, mutually orthogonal, and span the \(n\)-qubit Hilbert space.

The supplement consistently enumerates the binary basis labels from zero to
\(2^n-1\).

## Clifford basis update [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-clifford-update
Source locator: Article Main results item (1), Eqs. (3)--(4), PDF page 2
PDF page: 2
Claim: A Clifford gate updates the stabilizer basis by tableau conjugation while leaving the coefficient state \(\lvert\nu\rangle\) unchanged.

Equation (4), printed on the following page, explicitly states
\(G\lvert\nu\rangle=\lvert\nu\rangle\).

## Two-term non-Clifford update [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-two-term-nonclifford
Source locator: Article Main results item (2), Eqs. (5)--(6), PDF page 3; Supplemental Material Sec. I.B, Lemma 2 and Eqs. (7)--(16), PDF pages 2--3
PDF page: 3
Claim: For a two-term operator decomposition, the source maps the non-Clifford action to a basis update plus a multi-qubit Pauli-axis rotation acting on the coefficient state.

The rotation axes depend on the binary destabilizer and stabilizer labels in
the decomposition. The source does not claim that this pulled-back rotation
remains local in the coefficient tensor-network ordering.

## Multi-term unitary extension [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-multiterm-extension
Source locator: Supplemental Material Sec. I.B, Eqs. (17)--(19), PDF page 3
PDF page: 3
Claim: The published supplement gives a formal factorization of a unitary decomposition with more than two terms into a sequence of rotations of the same general type.

This formula is an addition of the published treatment. Supplemental Material
PDF page 10 nevertheless says that software implementation of unitaries with
arbitrary decomposition is future work. The formula is not a PEPS construction
or a generic tensor-network contraction bound.

## Observable expectation [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-observable-expectation
Source locator: Article Main results item (3), Eq. (7), PDF page 3
PDF page: 3
Claim: For a decomposed Pauli observable \(O=\alpha\delta_{\hat n}\sigma_{\hat m}\), the source evaluates \(\langle O\rangle\) as \(\alpha\langle\nu\rvert X_{\hat n}Z_{\hat m}\lvert\nu\rangle\).

The phase \(\alpha\) arises from the chosen basis decomposition and is retained
in the coefficient-state expectation.

## Selective Born outcome and source case split [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-selective-born-outcome
Source locator: Article measurement paragraph and Eqs. (8)--(9), PDF page 3; Supplemental Material Sec. I.C, Eqs. (24) and (27)--(29), PDF pages 4--5
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source chooses the positive or negative projective-measurement outcome with probabilities \(p_\pm=(1\pm\langle O\rangle)/2\) and gives outcome-dependent updates of the stabilizer basis and coefficient state.

For \(\hat n\ne0\), the supplement defines \(k\) as the first nonzero bit of
\(\hat n\), updates the basis, and uses a one-coefficient-qubit projector with
a nonunitary Pauli-axis transformation. For \(\hat n=0\), Eq. (27) instead
keeps the basis fixed and directly applies the diagonal coefficient
projection \((I\pm\alpha Z_{\hat m})/2\). The source's attempted uniform
rejoining of these cases is not accepted below.

## Measurement branch norm [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-measurement-branch-norm
Source locator: Supplemental Material Sec. I.C, Lemma 3 and normalization statement following Eq. (24), PDF page 4
PDF page: 4
Claim: Supplemental Lemma 3 states that the unnormalized selected branch has norm \(\sqrt{(1\pm\langle O\rangle)/2}\), so division by that norm yields a normalized conditional state.

The final physical norm agrees with the direct projector identity. The
supplement's claimed uniform coefficient-operation proof has two separate
defects recorded below and is not used to justify the norm.

## Measurement can increase coefficient bond [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-measurement-bond-risk
Source locator: Article paragraph following Eq. (9), PDF page 3
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source explicitly states that non-Clifford gates and measurements can introduce correlations between coefficient amplitudes that increase the tensor-network bond dimension \(\chi\).

It therefore does not establish monotone coefficient-bond reduction under
repeated measurement.

## T-state product example [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-t-product-example
Source locator: Article Eqs. (11)--(12) and surrounding example, PDF page 3
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source gives a product of single-qubit \(T\) states whose coefficient state is a bond-one MPS in the selected stabilizer basis even though its pseudo-stabilizer rank is maximal.

This example illustrates representation dependence; it is not a syndrome-
circuit benchmark.

## Operator-Schmidt bond bound [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-operator-schmidt-bound
Source locator: Supplemental Material Sec. III, Eqs. (40)--(42), PDF page 9
PDF page: 9
Claim: The source states that applying an operator of Schmidt number \(k\) to a state of Schmidt rank at most \(\chi\) gives a resulting Schmidt rank at most \(k\chi\).

The displayed expansion contains at most \(k\chi\) product terms, which is
sufficient for the upper bound even though the proof's stronger
Schmidt-decomposition wording is not generally valid.

## Routing bounds and connectivity [paper_fact]
Fact ID: stn-vor-routing-bounds
Source locator: Supplemental Material Fig. 1 and paragraph following Eq. (42), PDF page 9
PDF page: 9
Claim: For the source's rotation circuit on an MPS, the stated bounds are \(4\chi\) for the relevant adjacent-bond CNOT pattern and \(16\chi\) when SWAP routing is required; it says better-connected tensor-network geometries can reduce the bound at greater contraction cost.

These are per-operation rank bounds, not measured full-process runtime or
memory results.

## Undefined measurement index for the zero destabilizer label [literature_gap]
Fact ID: stn-vor-gap-measurement-zero-domain
Source locator: Supplemental Material Sec. I.C, Lemma 3 and Eqs. (24), (27), and (34), PDF pages 4--6
PDF page: 4
Claim: Lemma 3 states one \(P_k\widetilde R\) coefficient operation without assuming \(\hat n\ne0\), although \(k\), the first nonzero bit of \(\hat n\), and hence \(P_k\) are undefined when \(\hat n=0\); defining \(\hat i_k\equiv0\) does not create a physical qubit and conflicts with Eq. (34)'s use of \(\hat i_k=0\iff(\hat i+\hat n)_k=1\).
Gap scope: source_local

Equation (27) supplies a separate, well-defined \(\hat n=0\) projection with
no basis update. Therefore the measurement primitive can be retained only by
splitting the two cases rather than by accepting the lemma's uniform rule.

## Schmidt-decomposition wording [literature_gap]
Fact ID: stn-vor-gap-schmidt-wording
Source locator: Supplemental Material Sec. III, paragraph following Eq. (42), PDF page 9
PDF page: 9
Claim: The source does not justify its statement that the displayed \(k\chi\)-term product expansion is already a Schmidt decomposition merely because the operator factors form orthogonal operator bases.
Gap scope: source_local

Operator orthogonality does not imply mutual orthogonality of all vectors
obtained by acting on arbitrary Schmidt vectors. The product-term count still
supports the rank upper bound.
