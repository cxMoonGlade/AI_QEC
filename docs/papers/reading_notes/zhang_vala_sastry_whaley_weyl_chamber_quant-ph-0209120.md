## Executive summary [paper]
By applying the **Cartan decomposition to su(4)** the authors show the geometric structure of non-local
two-qubit gates is a 3-Torus (coords `[c1,c2,c3]`), reduce its redundancy with the **Weyl group** to a
tetrahedron (Weyl chamber `a+`) one-to-one with local-equivalence classes, prove a necessary-and-sufficient
condition for "perfect entanglers" (exactly half of all non-local gates), and show any two-qubit gate is
exactly simulable by ≤3 applications of a fixed two-body Hamiltonian + ≤4 local gates.

## Method (deep) [paper]
**Cartan decomposition of su(4) (Sec. II.B).** g = su(4) splits as g = k ⊕ p with
- **k = span (i/2){σ¹ₓ, σ¹ᵧ, σ¹_z, σ²ₓ, σ²ᵧ, σ²_z}** — the local part (single-qubit generators), Lie algebra
  of K = SU(2)⊗SU(2).
- **p = span (i/2){σ¹ₓσ²ₓ, σ¹ₓσ²ᵧ, σ¹ₓσ²_z, σ¹ᵧσ²ₓ, σ¹ᵧσ²ᵧ, σ¹ᵧσ²_z, σ¹_zσ²ₓ, σ¹_zσ²ᵧ, σ¹_zσ²_z}** (Eq. 7) —
  **the non-local part = the span of all 9 Pauli⊗Pauli (two-body) operators.** Here σ¹_α σ²_β = σ¹_α ⊗ σ²_β.

These satisfy the Cartan commutation relations [k,k]⊂k, [p,k]⊂p, [p,p]⊂k (Eq. 9). The **maximal Abelian
subalgebra (Cartan subalgebra)** contained in p is
> **a = span (i/2){σ¹ₓσ²ₓ, σ¹ᵧσ²ᵧ, σ¹_zσ²_z}**   (Eq. 10)

i.e. **{XX, YY, ZZ} as the three mutually-commuting canonical generators**. By Prop. 1 (G = K exp(a) K),
**any U ∈ SU(4)** decomposes (Eq. 11, repeated Eq. 16/29/35):
> **U = k1 A k2 = k1 exp{ (i/2)(c1 σ¹ₓσ²ₓ + c2 σ¹ᵧσ²ᵧ + c3 σ¹_zσ²_z) } k2**,   k1,k2 ∈ SU(2)⊗SU(2), ci ∈ ℝ.

`[c1,c2,c3]` lives on a 3-Torus (π-periodic in each ci); the **Weyl group** W(G,K) (generated by the
reflections sα, Eq. 15 — permutations / sign-flip-pairs of [c1,c2,c3]) reduces it to the tetrahedron
`a+ = OA1A2A3` (Fig. 2C). The local invariants G1, G2 (Makhlin, Eq. 28/30) are functions of `[c1,c2,c3]`.

**Physical generation (Sec. V.A).** For a Hamiltonian whose iH lies in a, the paper writes the canonical
**two-body interaction Hamiltonian** directly:
> **H = ½(c1 σ¹ₓσ²ₓ + c2 σ¹ᵧσ²ᵧ + c3 σ¹_zσ²_z)**   (Sec. V.A, the Ha form),  U(t)=exp(iHt) → flow [c1t,c2t,c3t].

For a general iH ∈ p there is a local k with Adk(iH) = iHa, Ha of the same XX/YY/ZZ form (Eq. 58/59) — so
**every two-body non-local interaction is locally equivalent to a Σ over XX,YY,ZZ.**

## The OBSERVABLE / metric [paper]
**Local invariants G1, G2** (Makhlin invariants, Eq. 28/30): functions of the spectrum of m(U)=
(Q†UQ)ᵀ(Q†UQ), equivalently of `[c1,c2,c3]`:
> G1 = cos²c1 cos²c2 cos²c3 − sin²c1 sin²c2 sin²c3 + (i/4) sin2c1 sin2c2 sin2c3
> G2 = 4cos²c1 cos²c2 cos²c3 − 4sin²c1 sin²c2 sin²c3 − cos2c1 cos2c2 cos2c3   (Eq. 30)

These are the **local-equivalence-class labels**: two two-qubit gates are local-equivalent iff (G1,G2)
(equivalently `[c1,c2,c3]` in a+) coincide. Worked points (Sec. III.A.1): CNOT/CZ → [π/2,0,0], (G1,G2)=(0,1);
SWAP → [π/2,π/2,π/2]; √SWAP → [π/4,π/4,π/4], (G1,G2)=(i/4,0). **Perfect-entangler test** (Thm 1): U is a
perfect entangler iff the convex hull of the eigenvalues of m(U) contains 0 (Thm 2 gives the explicit
[c1,c2,c3] inequalities; half of all non-local gates qualify).

## Findings + numbers [paper]
- Non-local two-qubit gates ↔ points of a tetrahedron (Weyl chamber, volume π³/24; 24 chambers in the cube).
- **Exactly half** of non-local gates are perfect entanglers (polyhedron LMNPQA2, volume π³/48).
- Any two-qubit gate is exactly simulable with ≤3 uses of a fixed two-body H + ≤4 local gates.
- Class anchors: [CNOT]=[π/2,0,0]; [SWAP]=[π/2,π/2,π/2]; [√SWAP]=[π/4,π/4,π/4]; Controlled-U = line [γ,0,0].

## M28/M33 EXTENSION — the asymmetric cross-pairs X⊗Y, Y⊗X (added 2026-06-29) [ours → DIRECT]

> Re-reading the same full text for the **asymmetric cross-pair** mechanisms M28=`coherent_xy_parasitic_coupling`
> (X⊗Y) and **M33=`coherent_yx_parasitic_coupling` (Y⊗X)**. These are NOT Cartan axes (the Cartan
> subalgebra a is only {XX,YY,ZZ}, Eq. 10) — so the M22/M23 Ising/Cartan license does NOT directly cover
> them. This paper grounds them DIRECTLY by two distinct routes, both transcribed verbatim below.

**Route 1 — Y⊗X is a basis element of the non-local part p of su(4) (Eq. 7, p.5, source char ~19540).**
Verbatim from `outputs/papers/quant-ph/0209120.txt`:
> `p = span (i/2){σ1_x σ2_x, σ1_x σ2_y, σ1_x σ2_z, σ1_y σ2_x, σ1_y σ2_y, σ1_y σ2_z, σ1_z σ2_x, σ1_z σ2_y, σ1_z σ2_z}`  (Eq. 7)
> "Here σx, σy, σz are the Pauli matrices, and `σ1_α σ2_β = σ1_α ⊗ σ2_β`."

So **`σ1_y σ2_x = Y⊗X` is the 4th of the 9 listed Pauli⊗Pauli basis elements** of the non-local part p
(`σ1_x σ2_y = X⊗Y` is the 2nd = M28). Both cross-pairs are bona-fide independent two-body interaction
generators — exactly on the footing the Cartan axes XX/YY/ZZ enjoy, just outside the maximal Abelian a.

**Route 2 — the generalized anisotropic exchange with an INDEPENDENT `Jyx · Y⊗X` coefficient (Example 2,
"Generalized exchange with cross-terms", p.15, Eq. 60, source char ~54031).** Verbatim:
> "Example 2 (Generalized exchange with cross-terms). Consider the generalized anisotropic exchange
>  Hamiltonian `H = ½(Jxx σ1_x σ2_x + Jyy σ1_y σ2_y + Jxy σ1_x σ2_y + Jyx σ1_y σ2_x)` discussed in [38]."
>  (eigenvalues Eq. 60: `½{±√((Jxx+Jyy)²+(Jxy−Jyx)²), ±√((Jxx−Jyy)²+(Jxy+Jyx)²)}`.)

This is the explicit, named, physically-motivated Hamiltonian in which **`Jyx σ1_y σ2_x = Jyx (Y⊗X)`
appears with its OWN independent coefficient** alongside XX/YY/XY — the M28/M33 cross-terms as standalone
tunable interaction axes. (The eigenvalue formula Eq. 60 even shows the cross-terms enter via `(Jxy−Jyx)`
and `(Jxy+Jyx)` — the X⊗Y vs Y⊗X *asymmetry* is physical, not gauge.) Combined with Eq. 7, **the operator
form `H_M33 = Jyx (Y⊗X)` (and `H_M28 = Jxy (X⊗Y)`) is theorem-grade grounded here** — two DIRECT statements
(basis-element + independent-coefficient cross-term) in one paper.

**Structural fact for M33 (Y⊗X), distinct from XX/YY/ZZ.** Y⊗X mixes one imaginary Pauli (Y) with one
real Pauli (X), so `Y⊗X` is **pure-imaginary and anti-symmetric** (real part 0; `(Y⊗X)^T = −(Y⊗X)`),
while Hermitian, traceless, and involutory (`(Y⊗X)²=I`). Contrast the Cartan pairs XX/YY/ZZ which are
**real-symmetric**. And `Y⊗X = (X⊗Y)^T` — the M33 axis is the transpose of the M28 axis (the two are
distinct operators, `‖YX−XY‖_F = 2√2 ≠ 0`). [Verified from-scratch on GPU, derivation-check
`m33_fe_derivation_check.py` 2026-06-29: `‖Re(YX)‖=0`, `‖YX−YX^T‖=4`, `‖YX+YX^T‖=0`, `(YX)²=I`, `Tr=0`.]
This is the M33-sharp negative control: an X⊗Y (transpose) / XX / YY / ZZ reference is caught by the
