## Executive summary [paper]

A driven, damped quantum system coupled to a vacuum reservoir is unravelled into single
"trajectories": between photon detections the state evolves under a **non-Hermitian effective
Hamiltonian** `H_eff` (norm-decaying); at a detection the state is **reset** by a jump operator
`R` (for a two-level atom: projected to the ground state) and re-normalized. The squared norm of
the no-jump state is the **delay function / waiting-time distribution** `P0(t)` — the probability
that no photon is emitted in `[0,t]` after a reset, whose negative derivative is the first-jump
(next-photon) density `I1(t)`. Because a two-level atom is reset to `|0>` (ground) on every
emission, it **cannot immediately re-emit** — it must be re-excited first — so the conditional
next-emission rate right after a jump is suppressed. This is exactly **photon anti-bunching**
(`g^(2)` rising from a suppressed value at short delay) and the associated **sub-Poissonian**
photon statistics `(Delta n)^2 < <n>` (Mandel `Q < 0`). The review derives all of this from the
exclusive/next-photon densities without solving the full ensemble master equation.

## Method (deep) [paper]

**Effective (no-jump) generator.** For the driven two-level atom (Sec. V.A), master equation
`===== PAGE 64 =====` "rho_dot = -i/hbar[HA,rho] - Gamma{sigma11 rho + rho sigma11} + 2 Gamma
sigma01 rho sigma10" (Eq. 176). Conditional no-jump evolution is generated by a non-Hermitian
`H_eff`:
- `===== PAGE 64 =====` "|psi(t)> = e^{-iHeff t/hbar}|psi(0)>" with "Heff = HA - i hbar Gamma"
  (Eqs. 178-179).
- General V-system form: `===== PAGE 21 =====` "the effective Hamilton operator Heff is a
  non-Hermitean operator" whose "real part of -i Heff is negative", so `Tr rho^(0)_A(t)`
  decreases — this is the survival probability decaying (Eq. 50, pages 21-22).

**Waiting-time / delay function P0(t) (EXTRACTION #1).** The square of the norm of the no-jump
state IS the delay function:
- `===== PAGE 28 =====` "the square of the norm of Eq. (66) is just the delay function"
  → `P0(t) = <psi^(0)_A(t)|psi^(0)_A(t)>` (Eq. 67).
- Operational definition of the interval distribution and its integral form:
  `===== PAGE 15 =====` "The negative differential of this survival probability describes the
  delay function I1(tau)", `I1(tau) = -dP0/dtau` (Eq. 30), and
  `===== PAGE 16 =====` "P0(tau) = 1 - integral_0^tau I1(tau') dtau'" (Eq. 31).
- Identity linking first-jump density to the survival probability (in this normalization):
  `===== PAGE 20 =====` "I1(t) = P0(t)" (Eq. 43) — i.e. the first-photon density equals the
  (negative-derivative-normalized) delay function; explicit closed form for the driven TLS:
  `===== PAGE 65 =====` "P0(t) = <psi_eff(t)|psi_eff(t)>" with a two-exponential form (Eq. 181),
  eigenvalues `lambda_{1/2}` from `sqrt(Gamma^2 - Delta^2 - Omega^2 - ...)` (Eq. 182).
- Explicit next-detection **rate** (waiting-time density up to detector efficiency beta):
  `===== PAGE 66 =====` "I1,beta(t) = 2 Gamma beta rho11(t)" (Eq. 185) — the instantaneous
  next-jump rate is proportional to the *excited-state* population `rho11(t)`.

**Exclusive n-photon density and factorization into waiting times.** The probability density that
photons are emitted at exactly `t1,...,tn` (and none between) factorizes into products of the
first-jump density:
- `===== PAGE 24 =====` "P0(t,tn)I1(tn,tn-1) ... I1(t1,0)" (Eq. 60), i.e. a renewal process
  built out of independent waiting times, valid because after each reset the state is the same.
  `===== PAGE 24 =====` "in most cases this state will be the ground state of the system and will
  be the same after each emission".
- Conditional next-photon density from the reset operator `R` and no-jump propagator `S`:
  `===== PAGE 23 =====` "the probability density that after an emission of a photon at time t0 the
  next photon will be emitted at t1" (Eq. 59).

**Reset / conditioned post-jump state (EXTRACTION #3).** At detection the state is projected by the
jump (lowering) operator and re-normalized:
- Two-level atom: `===== PAGE 64 =====` "for the normalized state after the detection of a photon
  |psi(t+)> = sigma01|psi(t)> / ||.|| = |0>" (Eq. 180) — **the atom is reset to the ground state
  `|0>` after every emission.**
- General reset (density-operator form) via the reset superoperator:
  `===== PAGE 22 =====` "R rho^(n)_A(t) = sum_i 2 Gamma_ii |0><i| rho^(n)_A(t) |i><0|" (Eq. 55),
  and `===== PAGE 31 =====` "R is the reset operator giving the state after a photon has been
  detected" (Eq. 76 context). Historically: `===== PAGE 25 =====` "the system is reset back to its
  ground state on photodetection which is of course a physically correct picture".

**Reset superoperator + no-jump propagator = the full unravelling.** The exclusive probability that
exactly `n` photons emitted at `t1..tn`: `===== PAGE 23 =====`
"pn(t1,...,tn) = trA{S(t,tn)R ... R S(t1,0) rho_A(0)}" (Eq. 58), with `S(t,t0)` the no-jump
CPTP-shrinking map `e^{-iHeff(t-t0)} . e^{iHeff^dagger(t-t0)}` (Eq. 53, page 22).

## The MECHANISM [paper -> ours]

**Physical picture of anti-bunching (EXTRACTION #2).** A driven two-level atom Rabi-oscillates;
its excited population — hence its instantaneous emission rate `I1 ~ 2 Gamma rho11(t)` (Eq. 185) —
grows from zero after a reset. An emission terminates the oscillation and **returns the atom to the
ground state**, so it must be re-excited before it can emit again:
- `===== PAGE 65 =====` "This oscillation is then terminated by the emission of a photon which
  brings the atom back to its ground state. Then the whole process starts again."
- Directly after a jump `rho11 = 0`, so by Eq. 185 the conditional next-emission rate is
  **zero at zero delay and rises with re-excitation** — the defining signature of anti-bunching.
- Explicit anti-bunching statement (three-level fluorescence, same mechanism):
  `===== PAGE 15 =====` "for short times tau we expect to see anti-bunching ... and this has been
  observed experimentally from trapped ions".
- The delay function IS the object that encodes this suppression: `===== PAGE 15 =====` "evaluates
  the delay function describing the distribution of delay times before the next emission occurs".

`[paper -> ours]` **The QEC analogue.** After an `|e> -> |g>` emission (a detected jump / reset),
the atom is in `|g>`, so the *conditional* probability of an immediate second emission drops to
zero and recovers only as the system is re-excited. Mapping to error records: a "flip event"
(jump) leaves the system in the reset (ground) state, so the **conditional next-flip rate DROPS
right after a flip** and must rebuild — the record is *anti-bunched / regularized in time*, not
positively clustered. This is the exact physics behind the attribution "post-emission conditional
flip rate drops / anti-bunching": it is a consequence of the deterministic reset `sigma01|psi> ->
|0>` (Eq. 180) plus the rate `I1 = 2 Gamma rho11` (Eq. 185) starting from `rho11 = 0`.

## The OBSERVABLE / metric [paper]

- **Waiting-time distribution / delay function** `I1(tau) = -dP0/dtau`, `P0(tau)` the survival
  probability (Eqs. 30-31, page 15-16); driven-TLS closed form Eq. 181 (page 65). This is the
  first-passage density between consecutive jumps — the primary single-trajectory observable.
- **Second-order (intensity) correlation** `g^(2)(t,tau)`: `===== PAGE 14 =====`
  "g(2)(t, tau) = <: I(t + tau)I(t) :> / <I(t)>^2" (Eq. 24). Anti-bunching = `g^(2)` suppressed at
  short delay (rising from a dip). Relation to the *any-photon* vs *next-photon* rates:
  `===== PAGE 67 =====` "2 Gamma rho11(inf) beta g(2)(t) = Ibeta(t)" (Eq. 190) and the renewal
  relation `===== PAGE 16 =====` "I(tau) = I1(tau) + integral ... I1(tau-tau')I(tau')" (Eq. 32 /
  Eq. 209).
- **Sub-Poissonian metric — Mandel Q (EXTRACTION #4).** `===== PAGE 17 =====` "for a
  sub-Poissonian field (Delta n)^2 < <n>", and the Mandel parameter `===== PAGE 17 =====`
  "QM(tau) = ((Delta n)^2 - <n>)/<n>" (Eq. 37), written via `g^(2)` in Eq. 38. Sub-Poissonian
  (variance below mean, `Q < 0`) is the counting-statistics fingerprint of anti-bunched, regular
  emission; the paper contrasts it explicitly with Poissonian `(Delta n)^2 = <n>` and
  super-Poissonian sources (page 17), the latter being the positively-clustered / bright-dark case.

## Findings + numbers [paper]

- **Reset makes the record a renewal process.** Because the post-jump state is the ground state and
  identical after every emission, the n-photon density factorizes into independent waiting times
  `P0 I1 I1 ... I1` (Eq. 60, page 24) — a mathematically exact renewal structure.
- **Next-jump rate tracks re-excitation.** `I1,beta(t) = 2 Gamma beta rho11(t)` (Eq. 185): zero
  immediately post-reset (`rho11 -> 0`), rising with Rabi re-excitation → the anti-bunching dip.
- **Driven-TLS closed forms** (used to plot the waiting-time and g^(2)):
  `===== PAGE 67 =====` "rho22(inf) = Omega^2 / (2 Omega^2 + 4 Gamma^2)" (Eq. 192) and the
  stationary `g^(2)` Laplace transform Eq. 193; next-photon transform Eq. 194
  `===== PAGE 68 =====` "I1,beta(z) = beta Omega^2 Gamma / ((z+Gamma)(z(z+2Gamma)+Omega^2(z+beta
  Gamma)))". Parameters used: `Omega = 5 Gamma`, `Delta = 0` (page 65-66).
- **Efficiency bridges next-photon to any-photon.** For imperfect counter efficiency `beta` the
  measured density interpolates: `===== PAGE 67 =====` "for decreasing counter efficiency the
  function ... approximates the 'any photon' probability ... proportional to the intensity
  correlation function g(2)(t)". A perfect (`beta -> 1`) 4-pi counter measures the true
  next-photon (waiting-time) density; `beta = 0.0049` from Mandel's anti-bunching experiment gives
  essentially `g^(2)` (page 66-67).
- **Super-Poissonian (opposite regime) for shelving V-systems.** With a metastable "dark" level
  the same formalism gives large fluctuations: `QM(tau->inf) = TD^2/TB <I>` (Eq. 39, page 17) and
  `g^(2)(tau) ~ 1 + (TD/TL) exp[-(1/TD+1/TL)tau]` (Eq. 208, page 72) — a **bunched / positively
  clustered** telegraph signal. Contrast: the bare driven TLS is anti-bunched/sub-Poissonian; the
  shelving system is super-Poissonian. (Both fall out of the same waiting-time machinery.)

## Limitations [paper]

- **Reset-to-ground is exact only for the two-level V-system.** For a Lambda configuration the
  post-jump state can be a *mixture*, so the trajectory must carry a density operator:
  `===== PAGE 28 =====` "the state after the emission can be a mixture (e.g. as in the three-level
  Lambda configuration)". So "anti-bunching = deterministic reset to |g>" is cleanest for a TLS.
- **Continuous measurement is idealized.** Requires coarse-graining `Delta t` between the field
  correlation time `omega10^-1` and the atomic timescales: `===== PAGE 26 =====`
  "Gamma_ii^-1, Delta_i^-1, Omega_i^-1 << Delta t" (Eqs. 62-63); too-frequent measurement gives
  the quantum Zeno effect (page 26).
- **Absorption of the photon is an added assumption**, not the projection postulate proper:
  `===== PAGE 29 =====` "not included in the projection postulate but enters as a physically
  justified additional assumption" (Eq. 69 context) — though ideal (non-absorbing) measurement
  gives the same result (page 28-29).
- **Correlation-function unravellings need care.** Single-run interpretation of `<A(t+tau)B(t)>`
  via the Molmer et al. four-wavefunction recipe is subtle (pages 62-63); ensemble averages are
  correct but conditioned single-run spectra require the Hegerfeldt-Plenio / Gardiner scheme.

## Relevance [ours]

This review is the **canonical, citable source** for the physics `qec_twin` invokes when it says
"after an e->g emission the conditional flip/re-excitation rate DROPS (anti-bunching)":
1. The **waiting-time distribution** `I1(tau) = -dP0/dtau` (Eqs. 30-31, 43; TLS Eq. 181) is the
   exact object for "time between jumps given a reset just happened" — the substrate-level analogue
   of the delay between detected error events in a trajectory-unravelled QEC error model.
2. The **reset state** `sigma01|psi> -> |0>` (Eq. 180) and the reset superoperator `R`
   (Eqs. 55, 76) are the formal statement that a jump projects the system to the jumped state and
   re-normalizes — directly matching our MCWF carrier's jump-and-renormalize step
   (`forward/scalable/mps_forward.py`, `sv_sampler.py`; ADR 0010) and the T1-MCWF √N rare-event
   note in memory.
3. The **anti-bunching / sub-Poissonian** contrast (`Q<0`, `(Delta n)^2 < <n>`, Eqs. 37-38) vs the
   **bunched/super-Poissonian** shelving telegraph (Eqs. 39, 208) is the metric language for
   distinguishing a *regularized* jump record (post-jump suppression) from a *positively clustered*
   one — precisely the discriminator needed when adjudicating whether an error record shows
   post-emission suppression or classical Poisson/positive-clustering.
4. `I1 = 2 Gamma rho11(t)` (Eq. 185) makes the mechanism quantitative: the conditional next-jump
   rate is proportional to the (re-)excited population, which is **zero right after a reset** — the
   theorem-grade statement behind "conditional flip rate DROPS after an emission".

## How to use / trust [ours]

- **Epistemic class (per METRICS.md / CLAUDE.md):** the reset `|psi(t+)> = |0>` (Eq. 180), the
  delay-function identity `I1 = -dP0/dtau` (Eq. 30), and `I1 = 2 Gamma rho11` (Eq. 185) are

  sub-vs-super-Poissonian variance (Eqs. 37-38), not an ad-hoc clustering statistic — run the
