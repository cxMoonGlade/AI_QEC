- **Key relationship to our work:** This is the foundational reference for the crux question itself — "does measurement suppress entanglement growth, and at what rate does it flip a circuit from volume-law to area-law." ## Executive summary [paper]

The paper defines a dynamical phase transition in 1+1D (and, more schematically, higher-D) quantum circuits made of local unitary gates interspersed with random projective measurements applied at rate `p` per site per time step. Below a critical rate `p_c`, the system is in an **entangling phase**: entanglement grows unboundedly ("ballistically", `S ~ t`) and the steady state has volume-law entanglement. Above `p_c`, the system is in a **disentangling phase**: entanglement saturates to a finite constant and only area-law entanglement survives in steady state. The transition is continuous, with `S ~ log t` exactly at `p_c`. The paper first solves an exactly-tractable toy model — the dynamics of the zeroth Rényi entropy `S0` (= log2 of the number of nonzero Schmidt values = the EXACT MPS bond dimension needed for a lossless representation) — by mapping it onto a classical bond-percolation minimal-cut problem, with the percolation threshold `p_c = 1/2` (2D square lattice) and correlation exponent `ν = 4/3`. It then studies the "generic" transition, visible in the physically relevant von Neumann/higher Rényi entropies (`S1`, `S2`, `S∞`), numerically via exact MPS simulation of random-unitary and Floquet circuits, finding a **lower** critical rate (`p_c ≈ 0.21–0.27`) and a **different** universality class (`ν ≈ 2.03 ± 0.05`) than the toy model. A key structural fact (their Footnote 13) is that between the generic `p_c` and the toy-model `p_c = 1/2`, the raw Schmidt rank `S0` can still grow linearly in time (volume-law) even while the physically relevant entropies `S1, S2, S∞` already saturate (area-law) — i.e. the "exact bond dimension" and the "physically-meaningful, truncatable bond dimension" are **not the same observable** and can disagree about which phase the system is in. Section VI.B explicitly draws out the implication for numerics: the entangling/disentangling transition determines whether an MPS-type simulation of a monitored quantum trajectory is exponentially hard or efficient.

## Method (deep) [paper]

- **Setup (Sec. II):** 1+1D chain of spin-1/2's, discrete-time circuit dynamics (running-bond brick-wall unitary layers, Fig. 3). After every layer of unitaries, each spin is projectively measured (in the `Sz` basis) independently with probability `p`. Measurement outcomes are drawn via the Born rule and the state is renormalized after each projection — dynamics are stochastic and nonlinear but the state remains **pure** (this is the trajectory/quantum-jump picture, not the averaged density matrix — Sec. II explicitly notes the averaged/mixed density matrix `ρ_av` is *trivial* [→ infinite-temperature] at any `p>0` and shows NO transition; the transition lives only in individual pure-state trajectories, diagnosed by nonlinear functions of the state such as entanglement entropy or the squared/variance-type correlator Eq. 7).
- **Two circuit ensembles studied:** (i) Haar-random 2-qubit unitaries per brick; (ii) a fixed deterministic **Floquet** unitary (Eq. 1, an Ising-type brick with both transverse and longitudinal fields, `φ_xx=0.3, φ_zz=0.6, θ_x=0.2, θ_z=0.4`) — chosen specifically to check the transition is not an artifact of randomness in the unitaries. Both give compatible universal behavior (same universality class), only the **nonuniversal** location `p_c` differs.
- **Entanglement observable:** Rényi entropies `S_n(A) = 1/(1-n) log2 Tr_A ρ_A^n` (Eq. 2), measured in bits; `n→1` gives von Neumann `S1`; `n→0` gives the Hartley/rank entropy `S0 = log2(number of nonzero Schmidt values)`; `n→∞` gives `-log2(λ_max)`. Ordering `S_∞ ≤ S_n ≤ (n/(n-1)) S_∞` (Eq. 4) forces all `S_n, n≥1` to share the same scaling with system size — hence a single "generic" transition for all `n≥1`.
- **The toy model / exact minimal-cut mapping (Sec. III, Fig. 4):** `S0` maps EXACTLY onto a classical bond-percolation optimization: each projective measurement "breaks" a bond in the space-time circuit graph (probability `p` per bond broken); `S0` = the minimal number of *unbroken* bonds a cut separating a subsystem from its complement must cross (first-passage percolation on the dual lattice). This mapping is exact for **any non-fine-tuned unitary** in this circuit geometry, holds for the Floquet circuit as much as for the Haar-random one, and generalizes to any number of spatial dimensions `d` (Sec. V) — the minimal cut becomes a `(d-1)`-dimensional minimal membrane. The critical rate is exactly the **bond percolation threshold of the corresponding space-time lattice** (`p_c=1/2` for the square lattice used in 1+1D; **lattice/dimension-dependent** in general — e.g. `~0.6` fraction of broken bonds for various 4-coordinated 3D lattices, refs [53–56]).
- **The generic transition (Sec. IV):** no exact classical mapping exists for `S_n, n>0` in general. Diagnosed purely numerically via exact-state MPS simulation (ITensor) of both circuit ensembles, `L` up to 24, using a finite-size scaling collapse `S_n(L,p) − S_n(L,p_c) = G̃((p−p_c) L^{1/ν})` (Eq. 16) with `p_c, ν` extracted by an unbiased objective-function search (Appendix A).
- **Control parameter:** `p`, the per-site-per-time-step probability of projective measurement — a **uniform, i.i.d.-random, all-sites** measurement rate. This is the single tuning knob of the whole paper.

## The regime boundary [paper → the crux]

This is the load-bearing section for our question.

**1. What makes entanglement bounded vs. growing, mechanically:** a projective measurement removes a degree of freedom from the entangling network ("breaks a bond" in the minimal-cut / percolation picture). If broken bonds are dense enough (`p>p_c`) that the *unbroken* bonds no longer percolate/span the system, any cut separating a subsystem from the rest needs only cross an O(1) number of unbroken bonds near the cut's anchor point — hence entanglement saturates (area law). If unbroken bonds still percolate (`p<p_c`), a cut must cross an unbroken-bond count that scales with time/size — hence linear/volume-law growth. **This mechanism is dimension- and lattice-independent in its logic**, but the numerical value of `p_c` is the bond-percolation threshold of the *specific* space-time lattice induced by the circuit's connectivity — it is NOT universal (1+1D square lattice: `p_c=1/2`; generic 3D four-coordinate lattices: `p_c~0.6` fraction broken; the paper gives no number for our specific 2+1D syndrome-circuit lattice).

**2. Two different critical rates for two different observables — the central subtlety.** The paper finds `p_c(generic, S_{n≥1}) < p_c(toy, S0) = 1/2` (e.g. `0.21-0.27` vs `0.5`). In the intermediate window `p_c(generic) < p < 1/2`, **`S0` (= log2 of the exact/untruncated Schmidt rank = the raw bond dimension needed for a lossless MPS) grows linearly with time (volume-law), while the physically-relevant `S1` (von Neumann), `S2`, `S∞` are ALREADY area-law/saturated** (their Footnote 13, derived directly from `S_n = [1/(1-n)] log2 Σ λ_i^n`: volume-law `S0` ⇒ number of *nonzero* eigenvalues diverges, but area-law `S_{n≥1}` ⇒ `Σ λ_i^n` stays finite for all `n≥1`). This means: **a huge number of Schmidt values can be nonzero (raw rank grows) while almost all of that weight is negligibly small and contributes essentially nothing to any physically-meaningful entropy or to any truncated/approximate representation.** This is the paper's own explicit statement that "exact bond dimension" (`S0`-type accounting, i.e. counting every nonzero singular value including numerically tiny ones) and "physically-meaningful/truncatable bond dimension" (`S1`-type, governed by the decay of the Schmidt spectrum) are **different observables that can disagree about which phase the system is in.**

**3. Where a high-measurement-rate (`p≈1`) circuit sits:** Both critical rates found in this paper (`0.21`–`0.5`, all model variants) are well below `1`. At `p` close to unity, the disentangling-phase formula (Eq. 11) gives a saturation value `S0(t→∞,p) ≃ A·ν·ln[1/(p−p_c)]`, which is a SMALL, slowly (logarithmically) varying number for `p` far above `p_c` — i.e. the paper's own scaling form predicts that a near-maximal measurement rate produces **rapid saturation to a small area-law plateau**, not growth. A syndrome-extraction circuit that measures essentially every ancilla every round sits deep in the "large `p`" regime of this phase diagram *by the paper's own logic*, strongly on the disentangling/area-law side.

**4. Clifford/stabilizer structure is a crucial special case the paper itself flags (Footnote 6):** "for certain types of unitary dynamics `S0` coincides with the von Neumann entropy. This includes dynamics generated by unitary gates in the Clifford group [41]." This means for a **purely Clifford/stabilizer circuit** (idealized Pauli-only syndrome extraction, no leakage), the toy model (`S0`, exactly `p_c=1/2` on the square lattice, exactly solvable via percolation) is not just a bound or an analogy — it is the **exact answer for the physical entanglement**, because for Clifford circuits `S0 = S1 = S_∞` identically. This is a load-bearing, model-class-specific fact: it says the "generic" universality class (`p_c≈0.21-0.27`, a genuinely different, harder-to-saturate transition) is a feature of **generic (non-Clifford, e.g. Haar-random or the specific chosen non-Clifford Floquet unitary) dynamics**, and does **not** directly apply to a Clifford-structured stabilizer circuit. For an idealized Pauli-syndrome-extraction circuit, the relevant threshold should instead be the (exactly solvable, and typically higher/easier-to-cross) percolation threshold, reinforcing that bond saturation at high measurement density is even more assured for Clifford dynamics than for the generic universality class studied numerically here.

**5. Structural/geometry caveats that could push toward growth (must flag against our crux):**
 - *Non-Clifford leakage breaks the exact `S0=S1` identity.* Our d5 carrier includes non-Pauli leakage (qutrit local dimension, non-Clifford gates) — this moves the effective dynamics away from the "Clifford ⇒ exactly percolation" special case and back toward (or beyond) the "generic" universality class, where `p_c` is *lower* and the transition is harder to reach — i.e. leakage is a plausible, paper-consistent mechanism that could push a nominally-high-`p` circuit closer to its (lower, but still nonzero and `<1`) threshold, not across it.
 - *The higher-D "generic" transition is unverified in this paper.* Section V's higher-dimensional generalization is stated and analytically argued **only for the `S0`/percolation toy model** — the physically relevant `S_{n≥1}` "generic" transition, with its own (lower, distinct-universality-class) `p_c`, is established numerically **only in 1+1D** (`L≤24` MPS). The paper does not claim, numerically or analytically, what `p_c(generic)` is in 2+1D. Extrapolating "high `p` ⇒ area law" from 1+1D to a 2+1D syndrome circuit is a plausible but UNVERIFIED extension in this specific paper (it is directly asserted as expected/likely — "similar considerations arise in higher dimensions", Sec. VI.B — but not shown).
 - *The measurement pattern in a real syndrome circuit is structured, not i.i.d.-uniform.* This paper's `p` is a spatially-uniform, temporally-i.i.d.-random measurement probability applied to *every* site independently each layer. A real d5 syndrome-extraction circuit measures a FIXED, deterministic subset of sites (ancillas only, and only once per round after several CNOT layers), never the data qubits directly, and does so exactly-periodically rather than stochastically. Mapping our circuit's "effective `p`" onto this paper's `p` is therefore an **analogy, not a literal correspondence** — a rigorous statement would require constructing the actual space-time percolation/minimal-cut network for our specific circuit graph (as the paper's own Sec. V / Fig. 16 machinery would let one do), not merely asserting "our circuit measures often, therefore p≈1."
 - *The paper only fully solves the exactly-tractable observable (`S0`) in the regime that matters least for us numerically.* The `S0`-type "raw rank" is the LESS relevant of the two observables for a truncated PEPS/MPS carrier — see point 2. If a pilot diagnostic is (even implicitly) tracking something closer to `S0` (e.g. counting all singular values surviving a very loose or improperly-calibrated truncation threshold) rather than a fidelity/entropy-controlled truncated bond dimension, the paper's own Footnote-13 mechanism predicts EXACTLY the artifact profile suspected: apparent linear growth in a regime where the physically relevant entanglement is already saturated.

## Relevance to the d5 PEPS crux [ours]

**Verdict: this paper is squarely supportive of "bond should saturate at high measurement rate (so our observed pilot growth is very likely an instrument artifact), not evidence for a genuine growth mechanism" — with two important, paper-grounded caveats that must be checked, not dismissed.**

- The core physics (measurement destroys entanglement by "breaking bonds" in the causal/tensor network; sufficiently dense measurement makes the unbroken-bond network fail to percolate, forcing saturation) is model-independent in its logic and directly supports expecting bond saturation for a circuit where essentially every ancilla is measured every round (`p` deep in the "large" regime relative to every threshold this paper reports, all of which are `≤ 0.5`, i.e. far below the `p≈1` regime our syndrome circuit operates in).
- The **Clifford special case (Footnote 6)** is a strong, directly-applicable result for the Pauli/stabilizer BULK of a syndrome-extraction circuit: `S0 = S1` exactly for Clifford dynamics, so the EXACTLY solvable percolation toy model (not just a heuristic analogy) governs the physical entanglement of the idealized (leakage-free) circuit, and its threshold (`p_c=1/2` on the relevant lattice, generically < 1 on any reasonable lattice) is comfortably below the effective measurement density of a syndrome circuit.
- **However**, the paper's own Footnote-13 mechanism is a precise, falsifiable candidate explanation for exactly the artifact we suspect: an inflated raw-rank/`S0`-type bond-count can grow even while the truncatable (`S1`-type) content saturates, if the diagnostic being tracked doesn't discriminate between "many tiny singular values" and "many singular values that matter at the operating truncation fidelity." A compiled weight-4 `√E_s` POVM (our suspected artifact source) is exactly the kind of object that can inject a large number of numerically-small but formally-nonzero singular values — precisely the failure mode this paper's own Appendix B.2 documents in ITensor's canonicalization ("a certain number of spurious eigenvalues, with value of order of the numerical precision, are in general retained, producing a systematic (positive) error in the calculation of `S0`" — but NOT in `S_{n≥1}`, which "do not suffer from this same source of inaccuracy, since small eigenvalues contribute very little to them"). **Actionable diagnostic implied directly by the paper: re-measure the pilot's bond growth using a fidelity/entropy-controlled truncated bond dimension (Sn≥1-consistent, e.g. keep singular values needed for 1-ε cumulative weight) rather than raw SVD rank / all-nonzero-singular-value count — if the apparent growth is a Footnote-13-type artifact, it should disappear or saturate much sooner under that diagnostic.**
- The paper does **not** establish the "generic" (non-Clifford) transition in 2+1D, so if leakage/non-Pauli structure pushes our circuit meaningfully away from the Clifford special case, we cannot cite this paper for a specific 2+1D `p_c` — only for the qualitative expectation (stated but not proven here) that high measurement density should still favor an area-law phase, generalizing the 1+1D numerics and the exactly-solved-in-any-`d` toy model.
- **Geometry dependence is real and unquantified for us**: `p_c` is a nonuniversal lattice/circuit-graph-dependent number (`1/2` square lattice vs `~0.6` for the 3D four-coordinate lattices cited). Our actual syndrome-circuit connectivity graph (CNOT ordering, ancilla degree, code distance d5) would need its own percolation-threshold calculation (using the paper's own Sec. V / Fig. 16 method) to get a quantitative, not just qualitative, threshold — this is an open task, not something this paper hands us directly.

## How to use / trust + open questions [ours]

- **Trust level:** FULL-TEXT 精读, 2541-line arXiv source (main text through references), founding PRX paper, heavily cited (>1000 citations in the MIPT literature as of the note's writing) and one of three simultaneous, mutually-confirming discovery papers (Chan-Nandkishore-Pretko-Smith; Li-Chen-Fisher — both cited in the paper's own "Note added"). The toy-model result (`S0`↔percolation, `p_c=1/2`, `ν=4/3`) is an EXACT theorem-grade mapping, proved for first-passage percolation in the cited Ref. [45]. The "generic" transition result (`p_c≈0.21-0.27`, `ν≈2.03`) is a numerically-established finding (finite-size scaling collapse, `L≤24` MPS, uncertainty-weighted), not an exact theorem — treat `p_c(generic)` and `ν(generic)` as **prediction-band-grade**, not exact-grade, per our own epistemic-status convention.
- **Independent-checkability:** the classical percolation `p_c=1/2`, `ν=4/3` for 2D square-lattice bond percolation are textbook-exact and independently verifiable trivially. The MPS numerics (`p_c(generic)`, `ν≈2.03`) would need independent reproduction (e.g. via our own `mps_forward.py`) to trust at more than "consistent with the founding paper" level for our own circuit.
- **Open questions for our build:**
  1. Is our actual pilot diagnostic tracking an `S0`-type raw rank (all nonzero singular values) or an `S1`/fidelity-controlled truncated bond dimension? This is the single most actionable check the paper suggests — re-run with a fixed-fidelity truncation and see if the apparent 4→18→>40 growth persists.
  2. What is the actual Clifford fraction of our d5 syndrome-extraction circuit (Pauli bulk vs. leakage/non-Clifford component), and does the Footnote-6 exact-percolation correspondence approximately hold for the bulk, with leakage treated as a perturbation that could shift the effective `p_c` upward (toward the "generic" class) but plausibly not past `p≈1`?
  3. Constructing the actual space-time percolation/minimal-cut network for our specific 2+1D circuit graph (per Sec. V / Fig. 16's method) would give a QUANTITATIVE threshold for our lattice rather than borrowing the 1+1D square-lattice number — this is a well-defined, tractable follow-up calculation, not requiring new physics beyond what this paper already sets up.
  4. The paper's own outlook (Sec. VI.D) flags exactly our situation as open: "it would be useful to make a more detailed simulation study of the quantum problem" for higher-dimensional/structured circuits — no citation exists in this paper for a 2+1D generic-transition threshold; a follow-up literature search (Gullans-Huse and successors on stabilizer/Clifford-specific MIPT, which explicitly targets exactly the Clifford case flagged in Footnote 6) is the natural next read to firm up point 2.
