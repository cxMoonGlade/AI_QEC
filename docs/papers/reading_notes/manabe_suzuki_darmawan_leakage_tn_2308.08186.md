## Executive summary [paper]

The paper presents **an MPS-based simulation method for leakage errors in QEC codes** without replacing the coherent leakage model by the Generalized Twirling Approximation (GTA). The quantum state (qutrits: |0>, |1>, |2>) is represented as an MPS in canonical form; two-qubit gates are applied as MPOs followed by SVD truncation (canonical update). The method is applied to two systems: (1) the **1D repetition code** of up to d=99 (~197 qutrits) with 99 rounds, and (2) a **thin 3xd surface code** of up to d=19, with a snake-like MPS ordering through the 3-wide strip (total ~6d-1 qutrits). The key enabler is the low entanglement generated by noisy repetitive stabilizer measurements: bond dimensions saturate over many rounds (Fig. 6), and the area law holds. They simulate declared leakage models (coherent control leakage, thermal noise, leakage spreading) and three leakage removal strategies. **Critical finding:** the GTA incoherent surrogate can **overestimate logical error rate by >3x** compared to the coherent model in the reported MLR repetition-code setting (Fig. 8). They also observe a non-monotonic interaction between coherent leakage and amplitude damping at low temperatures (Fig. 10).

**2026-07-13 closure boundary [paper -> ours].** Eqs. 14–20 construct an incoherent GTA surrogate
from the same coherent unitary's leakage/seepage rates `L1,L2`; Fig. 8 then shows that this rate match
does not fix the LER. This is direct published support for “`L1,L2` matching is insufficient.” The
headline comparison is nevertheless a **1D repetition-code** result under a phenomenological model.
The paper does not compare a full `d×d` surface-code record, does not insert pinching after quarter-CZ
slices, and reports neither full-record TV/KL/NLL nor a state-truncation-to-LER error theorem.

## Method (deep) [paper]

**Leakage noise model -- qutrit system (Sec. II):** Qubits are modelled as qutrits {|0>, |1>, |2>}. Four noise sources:

1. **Single-qubit gate errors (Sec. II.A):** Coherent leakage from fast control pulses. Modeled as Rabi oscillations between qubit and leakage states: `U = U_z(phi_i) * R_02(theta, lambda_i) * R_12(theta, lambda_i)` (Eq. 1-5), where R_02 and R_12 are rotations in the |0>-|2> and |1>-|2> subspaces. Random phases lambda_i, phi_i in [0, 2pi) per qubit. Theta = rotation strength.

2. **CZ gate errors (Sec. II.B):** Two effects when a qubit is in |2>: (a) **undesired phase shifts** -- control in |2> adds phase i (pi/2) to the target (Eq. 6); (b) **leakage spreading** -- CZ on a leaked qubit causes neighbors to leak, modeled as a sequence of Y-rotations in {|02>,|22>}, {|12>,|22>}, {|20>,|22>}, {|21>,|22>} subspaces with angle theta_spread. The noisy CNOT is `(I x H) CZ_noisy (I x H)`.

3. **Measurement (Sec. II.C):** Dispersive readout cannot distinguish |2> from qubit space: results are randomly 0 or 1 with equal probability (Eq. 8-9). Measurement leakage is neglected but "easily taken into account."

4. **Idling/thermal noise (Sec. II.D):** Harmonic oscillator coupled to thermal bath -- amplitude damping + thermal excitation via Lindblad master equation (Eq. 10) integrated over gate time. Parameters: qubit freq 10 GHz, tau=1.0 us, gamma in MHz, T in mK.

**MPS simulation method (Sec. III):** The state is an MPS in canonical form (Eq. 11, Fig. 1a). Two-qubit gates are applied as MPOs: the canonical gauge is centered on the first gate site ("top tensor"), the MPO is applied site-by-site, and SVD truncation restores the canonical form at each step (Fig. 1b, Appendix A). Bond dimension is chosen **dynamically** to keep truncation error below a threshold (10^-6 for repetition code, 10^-4 for thin surface code). Non-unitary noise (thermal, measurement) is simulated via **Kraus-operator sampling** (Appendix A, Eq. A9): a random Kraus operator K_i is selected with probability p_i = Tr(K_i |psi><psi| K_i^dag) and applied like a gate.

**QEC codes simulated (Sec. IV.A):**

1. **1D repetition code (Fig. 2):** d data qubits + d-1 ancillas = 2d-1 total. Only nearest-neighbor gates. MPS bond dimension saturates to a constant w.r.t. d (Fig. 6) -- area law holds.

2. **Thin 3xd surface code (Fig. 3a):** width=3, length=d. 6d-1 total qutrits. MPS snakes through the 3-wide lattice. Two-qubit gates require MPOs of **length up to 6** (Fig. 3b) -- the gate spans physical distance up to 3 sites in the snake ordering. This is the critical cost factor.

3. **Extensibility to wider strips (Sec. IV.A, ln 653-660):** "For the 5xd and 7xd surface codes... only bond dimensions chi=8,16 respectively are needed at most to represent the logical states... However, since the length of the MPO for the 2-qubit gates is long (10, 14 respectively), the effect of noise spreads over numerous tensors, resulting in an increase in the required computational resources." This is an explicit statement of the scaling challenge.

**Leakage removal strategies (Sec. IV.B):** Three strategies compared: (1) **No reset** -- only X gate on ancilla if outcome=1, leaked ancilla stays leaked (Eq. 12); (2) **MLR (multi-level reset)** -- perfect reset of ancilla to |0> after each syndrome measurement; (3) **DQLR (data-qubit leakage removal)** -- LeakageISWAP gate (Fig. 4) between data and ancilla after MLR, converting |02> to |11> in data, then MLR on ancilla again.

## The MECHANISM (for implementation) [paper -> ours]

- **Object:** pure-state MPS of dim `(d_q=3, chi)` for the qutrit quantum state (data + ancilla), evolved by unitary gates and sampled Kraus operators. The density matrix is **not** explicitly carried -- stochastic Kraus sampling replaces it. This is fundamentally a **state-vector trajectory MPS**, not a mixed-state PEPO.

- **Why the thin 3xd strip instead of full dxd (the decisive question):**
  - The MPS snakes through the 2D lattice. The "width" (smaller dimension) determines the bond dimension cost because the snake wraps across the width. A thin strip of width=3 is tractable because the snake only needs 3 "rows" of tensors between wraps.
  - With width=3, the longest-range gate spans at most 6 sites in the 1D snake ordering (Fig. 3b). The MPO has length 6, so at most 6 SVD truncations per gate.
  - For width=5 (5xd), the MPO length would be 10; for width=7, length 14. Each additional SVD step multiplies cost.
  - While the logical state itself needs only chi=4 (3xd), chi=8 (5xd), chi=16 (7xd), the **noise spread** across the longer MPO is what drives cost (ln 657-660).
  - **They never attempted full dxd.** The paper explicitly reserves that for _future work with PEPS/isoTNS_ (ln 564-566, ln 1441-1443).

- **Computational resources reported:**
  - **1D repetition code (d=99, 99 rounds):** average bond dimension ranges from ~3.73 (DQLR, weak noise) to ~64.83 (No reset, strong coherent noise) -- Fig. 5. Truncation error 10^-6.
  - **Thin 3xd surface code (d=7, 7 rounds):** average bond dimension ranges from ~9.1 to ~21.1 (Fig. 7). Truncation error 10^-4.
  - **Scaling vs rounds:** bond dimensions increase linearly at initial rounds, then **saturate** (Fig. 6) -- the system obeys an area law in the circuit time direction.
  - **Hardware:** single CPU node (Intel Xeon Platinum 9242, 96 threads). TensorNetwork Python library (Ref [68]). For d>=5, "the cost of performing SVD becomes a crucial bottleneck" (ln 1297-1298). They note parallel SVD (GPU/cluster) as future work.
  - **Sampling:** 10,000 samples per data point.

- **What breaks at full dxd:**
  - The snake MPS bond dimension scales **exponentially in width W** (the smaller dimension). For width W, the snake wraps across W sites each turn, so the bond between wraps must carry entanglement equivalent to cutting W bonds of the 2D lattice. Even if each cut carries chi~O(1), the combined bond is `chi^W`.
  - The MPO length scales as `2W` (gate spans W sites). Each SVD truncation in the MPO application sequence compounds truncation error.
  - For a dxd surface code with d>=7: `W=d=7`, MPO length 14, and the bond dimension at the waist of the MPS would explode. The authors did not attempt this.
  - Their explicit statement (ln 564-566): "Extending these methods to codes with more complex connectivity in two or higher dimensions will likely require tensor network ansatz beyond MPS, which we leave to future work."

- **Leakage model specifics relevant to our architecture:**
  - **Coherent control leakage** is the critical non-Pauli element: it is unitary transfer between computational and leakage subspaces, and the GTA incoherent surrogate overestimates logical error rate by >3x in the reported MLR case (Fig. 8). This directly requires validating GTA for a target model/schedule; it is not a universal error-direction theorem.
  - **Leakage spreading** (CZ on leaked qubit making neighbors leak) is modeled as a coherent process -- also not Pauli.
  - The interaction between coherent leakage and amplitude damping creates **non-monotonic** logical error rates (Fig. 10): at small gamma (weak damping), leaked states persist, causing higher logical error for larger d. At moderate gamma, damping removes leakage population, improving logical error rate. This counterintuitive behavior is invisible in small-system simulations.
  - These results are only obtainable because their method handles exact coherent dynamics.

## The OBSERVABLE / metric [paper]

- **Logical error rate** (the primary metric) via MWPM decoding after d rounds of syndrome extraction. Defined as probability of decoding failure (for repetition code: bit-flip of logical; for thin surface code: separately P_ZL and P_XL).
- **Average MPS bond dimension** at end of each round (Fig. 5, 6, 7) as a proxy for computational cost.
- **Truncation error:** 2-norm of vector of truncated singular values (threshold 10^-6 for repetition code, 10^-4 for thin surface code).
- **Leakage rate L1** and **seepage rate L2** (Eq. 14-15, Ref [70]) as diagnostic metrics for the GTA comparison.
- **Regime where informative:** the MPS is accurate where entanglement is bounded (noisy stabilizer measurements create area law). At very low noise or very high coherent leakage, bond dimensions grow and truncation error accumulates. The threshold of 10^-4 for the thin surface code is a weaker guarantee than 10^-6 for the repetition code.

## Comparison with Darmawan-Poulin (1607.06460) [paper vs paper]

This paper follows directly from Darmawan & Poulin (2017, Phys. Rev. Lett. 119, 040502) -- Ref [17] in the text. Key differences:

| Aspect | Darmawan-Poulin (2017) | This paper (Manabe et al. 2023/2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Full 2D tensor network (tensor-contraction approach) for the surface code under general noise | **1D MPS** with snake ordering, applied to the thin 3xd strip and 1D repetition code |
| Noise model | Non-Pauli coherent noise (not leakage specifically) | Full qutrit leakage model: coherent control leakage, thermal noise, leakage spreading |
| Syndrome measurements | **Noiseless** syndrome extraction (perfect measurements, "limited to the case with noiseless syndrome measurements" -- ln 453-455) | **Noisy** syndrome measurements with leakage included, multiple rounds |
| Code geometry | Conventional dxd surface code (arbitrary d) | Thin 3xd strip (fixed width=3, variable length) + 1D repetition code |
| Computational cost | Tensor network contraction cost grows with d but the approach was more limited in scale | MPS SVD bottleneck grows with strip width -- d>=5 already challenging |
| Leakage removal strategies | Not applicable | Three strategies compared (No reset, MLR, DQLR) |

**The critical difference:** Darmawan-Poulin 2017 could treat the full 2D surface code but only with **noiseless** syndrome measurements (the measurement itself was the noise-free, no-leakage ideal). Manabe et al. extend to realistic noisy+leaky measurements but **restrict to thin strips** because the MPS ansatz cannot handle full 2D. The two methods are complementary: the full 2D tensor network handles geometry, the MPS handles dynamics/noise -- but **neither does both at scale**.

## Limitations [paper]

- **MPS is 1D -- the thin strip is a workaround, not a solution for full 2D.** The paper is honest about this: "Extending these methods to codes with more complex connectivity in two or higher dimensions will likely require tensor network ansatz beyond MPS" (ln 564-566). The thin 3xd strip is chosen because its quasi-1D structure keeps the MPS tractable.

- **No full 2D surface code results.** All 2D claims about threshold disappearance under coherent leakage are **extrapolated** from the thin 3xd strip, not directly simulated on a dxd code (ln 1287-1291). The authors note this limitation.

- **CPU-only, one node.** For d>=5 on the thin 3xd surface code, SVD is already the bottleneck (ln 1297-1302). No GPU acceleration or distributed parallelism demonstrated.

- **Stochastic Kraus sampling, not density-matrix evolution.** The method samples individual Kraus operators per trajectory rather than evolving a mixed state. This is necessary for MPS (pure-state) simulation of non-unitary noise, but it requires many samples (10,000 per data point) and cannot directly compute observables from the mixed state.

- **Leakage spreading not simulated for the thin surface code** (ln 1275-1278): "Due to the simulation cost and the complexity of the procedure, we didn't consider leakage spreading, and only No reset and MLR strategies were adopted for the leakage removal." This is a nontrivial omission since leakage spreading is one of the most damaging effects in real hardware (Ref [6], [7]).

- **Truncation error threshold 10^-4** for the thin surface code vs 10^-6 for the repetition code -- a weaker guarantee, and they only verify convergence for the parameter regions studied, not universally.

- **No bond dimension data for d>7 on the thin surface code.** Fig. 7 only shows d=7. The d=11 and d=19 logical error rate results (Fig. 12) don't report bond dimension or truncation fidelity.

- **No error bars on logical error rates beyond** "ten thousand samples per data point" -- and for DQLR with d=99,49, "exactly zero logical errors were detected during the simulations" (Fig. 11 caption), meaning the results are censored at the sampling floor.

- **Simple Pauli+ is more efficient when it works** (ln 637-639): "When the noise contains Pauli and a certain simple type of incoherent leakage error, it can also be easily simulated using an extended Clifford simulator, such as Pauli+." The MPS method is only needed when coherent/non-Pauli effects matter.

## Relevance to qec_twin [ours]

### CRITICAL: What this paper says about "2D iPEPO at d=7 is feasible"

This paper provides **the strongest available evidence that MPS-on-full-dxd is infeasible** and explicitly points to PEPS/isoTNS as the needed generalization. Key points:

1. **The thin 3xd strip is the maximum for MPS.** The authors chose width=3 precisely because MPS cannot handle width >= d for a full dxd surface code. The MPO length (2W for snake ordering) creates a compounding truncation bottleneck.

2. **Darmawan-Poulin 2017 already did the full tensor-contraction approach for dxd codes** -- but only with noiseless syndrome measurements. Manabe et al. extend to noisy+leaky but retreat to the thin strip. **Neither approach combines a full 2D geometry with noisy multi-round dynamics at scale.**

3. **Explicit call for PEPS (ln 1441-1443):** "To investigate codes with higher dimensional structure, more sophisticated tensor-network algorithms will likely be required, such as PEPS and isoTNS." This is the paper's own forward-looking conclusion.

### What this means for our pseudomode-on-2D-iPEPO architecture

- **The MPS limit is a problem we already identified, and this paper confirms it.** Our choice to pursue a 2D PEPO (iPEPO) carrier for the surface code geometry is the correct direction given the MPS wall.

- **But this paper also highlights the gap between what's been demonstrated and what we need:**
  - The most advanced demonstrated 2D TN surface code simulation with realistic noise is the **thin 3xd strip, d <= 19, CPU-only, 10^-4 truncation, no leakage spreading**.
  - A **full dxd surface code at d=7 with multi-round noisy+leaky dynamics** has not been demonstrated by anyone using any tensor network method.
  - The 2D iPEPO papers (Dunham et al., arXiv:2512.01781) and PEPS approaches (Darmawan-Poulin 2017) each cover one side of the geometry-vs-noise divide, but not both at useful scale.

- **Concrete challenges from this paper for our architecture:**
  1. **Bond dimension growth from noise:** Even the thin 3xd strip (width=3) needs chi up to ~21 for d=7 with coherent leakage (Fig. 7). A full PEPO on dxd at d=7 would need significantly larger D -- and the truncation is uncontrolled.
  2. **SVD bottleneck:** This paper identifies SVD as the bottleneck already for the thin strip at d>=5 on a 96-thread CPU. A 2D PEPO requires far more SVD operations per truncation step (4 bonds per site vs 2 in MPS). The itrSU method (Dunham et al.) helps but is still uncontrolled.
  3. **Non-Pauli leakage is a cost multiplier:** Coherent leakage increases entanglement relative to Pauli/incoherent noise (Fig. 5: coherent parameters push bond dim from ~4 to ~65 in worst case). Our pseudomode approach adds more non-Pauli structure, potentially further increasing bond dimension.
  4. **The thin strip's saturation observation gives hope:** Bond dimensions saturate over rounds (Fig. 6). If this area-law-in-time behavior holds for PEPO-based simulations, many-round simulations could be feasible even if single-round bond dims are moderate.

- **Reassessment of feasibility tier:**
  - MPS + thin 3xd strip at d=7: **DEMONSTRATED** (this paper, d=7 with 7 rounds, CPU)
  - MPS + thin 3xd strip at d=19: **PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED** (logical error rates shown in Fig. 12, but no bond dimension reporting, and SVD bottleneck flagged for d>=5)
  - Full dxd surface code with MPS: **INFEASIBLE** (exponential-in-width bond dim)
  - Full dxd surface code with 2D PEPO/PEPS at d=3-5, Markovian-only noise: **PLAUSIBLY DEMONSTRABLE** (extrapolating from Dunham et al. tePEPO benchmarks + Darmawan-Poulin 2017 tensor contraction)
  - Full dxd surface code at d=7 with non-Markovian pseudomodes and multi-round noisy dynamics: **UNKNOWN** -- no published demonstration at this scale, and this paper's SVD bottleneck warning plus the uncontrolled truncation creates significant risk.

- **The most important datum this paper adds for our design:** Fig. 6 shows the **area-law-in-time** property holds for noisy QEC -- bond dimensions saturate regardless of number of rounds. This is the property that makes a tensor network approach (whether MPS or PEPO) viable at all. If we can keep single-round bond dimensions manageable (which requires a good truncation scheme), the time dimension does not compound indefinitely.

- **The countervailing warning:** The thin strip still needed 10^4 samples per point and could not include leakage spreading for the surface code (ln 1275-1278). If the _simplest_ 2D leakage simulation (width=3, no leakage spreading, CPU-cluster) already hits these limits, a full 2D PEPO with pseudomodes (higher local dim, more entanglement) is a significantly harder problem.

## How to use / trust + open questions [ours]

- **Trust level:** FULL-TEXT 精读 (1918 lines, 15 pages including Appendix A). Figures cross-referenced against fine-grained line-number data. The paper is published in NJP (peer-reviewed) and the first author's previous work (Darmawan-Poulin 2017) is independently verified.

- **Independent-orability:**
  - The 1D repetition code results could be independently verified with a TEBD/MPS simulator (e.g. ITensor, qec_twin's mps_forward.py) using the same noise parameters.
  - The 3xd surface code results would require an MPS simulator with snake-ordering support and the specific qutrit noise channels.
  - The GTA vs coherent-model comparison (Fig. 8) is the strongest check: it is a leakage-specific counterexample to treating rate-matched incoherent dynamics as automatically LER-equivalent.
  - The bond dimension scaling (Fig. 6) could serve as a convergence diagnostic check.

- **Open questions for implementation:**
  1. If we build a 2D iPEPO carrier (following Dunham et al.), does the area-law-in-time property (Fig. 6) **transfer** from MPS to PEPO? Or does the PEPO's higher representational capacity mean entanglement grows differently?
  2. The paper's SVD bottleneck for d>=5 (even on the thin strip) suggests that our pseudomode-augmented PEPO would face a **more severe** SVD bottleneck due to larger local dimension (d=4 or more for system+pseudomode vs d=3 here). Is the iterative simple-update (itrSU) of Dunham et al. sufficient, or do we need full environment truncation?
  3. Can we replicate the paper's thin-strip results as a **validation step** before moving to full 2D? If our MPS code reproduces Fig. 12 for the 3xd surface code, that builds trust in our leakage model before tackling the harder PEPO problem.
  4. The paper shows GTA **overestimates** the LER by more than 3x in one MLR repetition-code regime. That direction is source- and regime-specific; it does **not** make incoherent leakage approximations generally conservative. Our architecture must measure both magnitude and sign against its own exact reference.
  5. **The leakage spreading omission for the surface code (ln 1275-1278) is the biggest gap** between their simulation and real hardware. Our architecture must handle it. Is the LeakageISWAP-based DQLR protocol sufficient, or do we need a more sophisticated approach?
  6. The CPU-only 10^4 samples per point is a runtime concern. Using GPU-accelerated MPS (or PEPO) evolution, can we reduce wall-clock time to make parameter sweeps practical?

- **Bottom-line verdict for our architecture:**
  This paper **confirms the MPS-2D infeasibility** that motivated our PEPO direction, and **supports the need for PEPS/isoTNS** to handle full 2D surface code geometry with non-Pauli noise. However, it also acts as a **sobering constraint**: the simplest 2D leakage simulation (3xd, width=3, no leakage spreading, CPU cluster) already finds SVD to be the bottleneck at d>=5. A full 2D PEPO at d=7 with pseudomodes is a significant step beyond any published demonstration. The paper's area-law-in-time finding (Fig. 6) is the most encouraging signal: if bond dimensions saturate in time for the 2D case as they do for the 1D case, many-round simulations are tractable. But **the bond dimension cost per site and the SVD truncation quality remain uncontrolled risks** that we must characterize with small-scale benchmarks before committing architecture to a d=7 target.
