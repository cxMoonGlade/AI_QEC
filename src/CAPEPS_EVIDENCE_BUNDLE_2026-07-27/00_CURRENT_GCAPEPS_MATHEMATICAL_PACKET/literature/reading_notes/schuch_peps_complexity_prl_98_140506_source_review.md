# Full-text review — Schuch et al., “Computational Complexity of Projected Entangled Pair States”

## Source identity [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-source-identity
Source locator: Title block and publication metadata, p. 1
PDF page: 1
Claim: Norbert Schuch, Michael M. Wolf, Frank Verstraete, and J. Ignacio Cirac authored this Physical Review Letters article, published on 4 April 2007 as volume 98, article 140506.

The version of record gives DOI `10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.140506`.

## PEPS construction [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-peps-definition
Source locator: “PEPS and postselection,” p. 1, right column
PDF page: 1
Claim: The PEPS construction starts from an arbitrary undirected graph, places dimension-`D` maximally entangled virtual pairs along its edges, and applies a local linear map from the incident virtual spaces to a dimension-`d` physical space at every vertex.

The source usually associates the graph with a two- or higher-dimensional physical lattice, but its definition is stated for an arbitrary undirected graph.

## Postselected circuit encoded as a PEPS [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-postselected-circuit-to-peps
Source locator: “PEPS and postselection,” p. 2, left column, first two paragraphs
PDF page: 2
Claim: The output of a postselected quantum circuit can be translated efficiently through a two-dimensional cluster-state measurement construction into a square-lattice PEPS with `D=d=2` and size polynomial in the circuit length.

The construction fixes measurement outcomes that avoid Pauli corrections and postselects the circuit's designated output condition.

## PEPS generated by postselection [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-peps-to-postselection
Source locator: “PEPS and postselection,” p. 2, left column, third paragraph
PDF page: 2
Claim: The source states that any PEPS can be generated efficiently by a postselected quantum computer by implementing each local linear map as a selected element of a POVM.

The version of record presents the local-map construction at this level of detail.

## Postselection--PEPS duality [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-postselection-peps-duality
Source locator: “PEPS and postselection,” p. 2, left column, summary paragraph
PDF page: 2
Claim: The postselection--PEPS duality consists of efficient transforms in both directions between postselected circuit outputs and PEPS, with the circuit-to-state direction already realized by two-dimensional `D=d=2` PEPS.

The source uses this duality for its following computational-complexity arguments.

## Exact PEPS scalar primitives [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-peps-primitives
Source locator: “The classical complexity of PEPS,” p. 2, right column; note [16], p. 4
PDF page: 2
Claim: The paper-defined exact PEPS primitives are the norm, an unnormalized expectation value, and a normalized expectation value, and the source relates them by weakly parsimonious reductions.

Note [16] supplies the transformations between the three scalar computations.

## PEPS simulation hardness [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-peps-hardness
Source locator: “The classical complexity of PEPS,” p. 2, right column, final paragraph
PDF page: 2
Claim: For a polynomial Boolean function `f`, the source encodes a circuit preparing a sum over `|x>` and `|f(x)>` as a PEPS and uses the normalized expectation value of `sigma_z` on the output register to recover the satisfying-assignment count, establishing `#P`-hardness.

This is a reduction for the paper's scalar PEPS simulation problem.

## PEPS simulation membership [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-peps-membership
Source locator: “The classical complexity of PEPS,” p. 3, left column, first paragraph
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source argues membership in `#P` by expressing the PEPS norm as a postselection-success probability, expanding it over pairs of Toffoli/Hadamard computational paths, and reducing the resulting `{0,+1,-1}`-valued sum to satisfying-assignment counts.

The displayed path-pair expression supplies the probability before postselection, and the following Boolean function converts the signed sum into counting queries.

## PEPS simulation completeness [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-peps-completeness
Source locator: “The classical complexity of PEPS,” p. 3, left column, first paragraph; note [16], p. 4
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source concludes that the paper-defined exact PEPS primitives are `#P`-complete under weakly parsimonious reductions.

The conclusion combines the Boolean-circuit hardness reduction with the path-sum membership argument and the reductions among norm and expectation values.

## General tensor-network contraction completeness [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-general-tn-completeness
Source locator: “The classical complexity of PEPS,” p. 3, left column, second paragraph
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source concludes that general tensor-network contraction is `#P`-complete, using PEPS contraction for hardness and constructions based on `T` with its conjugate, a physical dimension-one PEPS norm, and tensor direct sums for membership.

The short reconstruction explicitly discusses the magnitude and sign of the real part of the scalar contraction.

## Approximation-hardness transfer [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-approximation-transfer
Source locator: “The classical complexity of PEPS,” p. 3, left column, third paragraph
PDF page: 3
Claim: The source states that linear postprocessing transfers its reductions to the corresponding approximation problems, so approximating the three PEPS primitives is as hard as approximating counting problems can be.

The paragraph gives separate sketches for normalized expectation values and for norm or unnormalized expectation values.

## Artificial PP-encoding states [paper_fact]
Fact ID: schuch-artificial-pp-states
Source locator: “The power of creating ground states,” p. 3, right column, opening paragraph
PDF page: 3
Claim: In its ground-state-oracle discussion, the source describes the PEPS that encapsulate PP problems as quite artificial.

The paragraph contrasts those states with PEPS used in connection with physical ground states.

Fact ID: schuch-gap-reverse-transform-input

Gap scope: source_local

## Exact membership precision bridge not expanded [literature_gap]

Gap scope: source_local

The article states the gate-set approximation and then performs the exact path-counting reduction.

## Approximation error semantics not specified [literature_gap]
